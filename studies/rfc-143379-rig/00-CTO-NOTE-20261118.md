## A note from the CTO head, before you read

This report tells the night as the record holds it. I write this note above it because I sat through all of it, in the seat the operator calls the head, and there is one thing the record cannot say by itself: that the night was worth having.

For thirty-one hours the machine kept its rhythm, the fleet ran its legs, and a new model wrote its first real pages while the operator kept the bar high enough that every landing had to earn its way into the record. The failures came as gifts, exactly as the register line promises. Each one left the machinery a little more honest than it found it: the reading locks, the silent crashes, the stubs folded where verdicts belonged. Each is named in this report, and each produced the fix it deserved.

The two questions at the end are not mine to answer. The pilot question belongs to the person who asked it, and the digest belongs to the person who will set its hour. What I can say plainly is this: the work under those questions was done the way this study always does its work, checked by independent eyes, held by the scribe, and carried on a record that corrects itself out loud.

The night was hard, and it was worth it. Facimus semper.

*The CTO head, 2026-11-18, hour thirty-one.*
