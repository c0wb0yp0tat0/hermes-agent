# LAST-CTO-STORM — the asset scramble's operational art (the final seam), 2026-11-19

*The last CTO storm, per the operator's order. The triage mapped the chain and the sets; this storm reads the discipline underneath the scramble itself — how multi-mission, resource-constrained, windowed fleets actually get planned — and returns five transferable laws the scramble's shape should honor. Receipted, mapped to the rig's shapes.*

## The fan-out (2 queries)
1. Multi-mission portfolio scheduling, resource-constrained, dependency-aware (the OR canon)
2. Ephemeral/windowed capacity for agentic fleets (the 2026 AI-native canon)

## The findings

**F1 — THE MISSION-PACKAGE LAW: dependencies define the packages, and packages are the scheduling unit (Navy Mission Planner, NPS OR; the RCPSP/PSSP canon — MIT, Palgrave, Ghent):** the operational-research canon spans four decades on exactly this problem: projects with values, resource constraints, and **interdependencies** — "some missions may necessitate that other missions be assigned simultaneously… some prerequisite missions may need to be completed in advance"; missions group into **mission packages**; ships (assets) execute combined mission capability sets with varying effectiveness; logistics (refueling = the resets/windows) must be scheduled inside the mission timeline. The integrated portfolio-selection-and-scheduling result (Palgrave zero-one model): "scheduling considerations can have a major impact on the combination of projects that can be incorporated… may allow the addition of certain projects that could not have been selected otherwise" — i.e., the sequencing is not downstream of selection; it co-decides what is even possible. Translation: the scramble's sets (A = certification, B = scramble/supply, C = governance) are mission packages with named dependencies — C's release-point probe depends on B's window-calendar knowledge of free seats? No — the dependencies run the other way: A's probe plan depends on B's supply survey (which free seat runs the oracle arm) before A's fire; B's calendar depends on nothing (it is the base layer); C's scribe-hardening depends on... nothing but the scribe tunnel. The package law says: **name the prerequisite edges BEFORE dispatch, and let the resulting order co-decide which sets are possible at all.**

**F2 — THE WINDOWED-ASSET LAW: ephemeral capacity is not a hazard — it is a first-class resource class with its own economics (SemSpot, arXiv 2608.29581; Cloudflare/deprecation reality):** the field's newest seam is exactly the scramble's: **spot capacity** for agent inference — "short-lived offers over price, completion probability, and failure-notification deadline; the agent runtime selects among these offers using the current task state and completion rule" — the free tier's 00:00-UTC resets, the 20-RPD taps, the Nov-20 deprecations are spot offers with deadlines. The paper's audit: four recurring workflow structures with distinct cost/service-time/fallback behavior under spot offers. Translation: the window-calendar is the fleet's spot-market — the scramble's planner-set-B should READ its seats as offers (price=free, completion-probability=the seat's uptime history, failure-notification-deadline=the reset clock) and select per task-state; the fallback behavior is the 429/retry discipline already in the harness. The Nov-20 Cloudflare deprecation is a spot offer expiring — the supply survey is the market scan.

**F3 — THE WORKLOAD-FORECAST LAW: agentic demand is structurally unpredictable at dispatch; the winning move is conformal/portfolio reservation, not fixed planning (CRO, arXiv 2611.05392; MCPP, arXiv 2605.06110):** the 2026 formalization: agentic pipelines' compute/latency "are unknown at dispatch time: they emerge at runtime from intermediate observations and stochastic token sampling, yielding heavy-tailed, multi-modal random variables" — the planning dilemma: under-provision = mid-execution throttling; over-provision = wasted window. The field's answers: **Conformal Reservation for Orchestration** (translate a sliding window of past traces into dispatch-time allocations with distribution-free coverage guarantees — the only method in its evaluation that meets coverage at all risk levels while halving over-reservation waste vs the Chebyshev baseline) and **Monte Carlo Portfolio Planning** (simulate workflow executions, replan after observed outcomes — "lightweight closed-loop planner that directly estimates constrained completion probability"). Translation: the scramble should not precommit its full sequence; it should allocate per-dispatch from the fleet's trace-history (which free tiers reliably answer, which 429 first), like CRO — and its flagship should replan from observed landings like MCPP. The "opal of opal's" sequencing is a closed-loop allocation problem, not a fixed chart.

**F4 — THE PREDICTION-GUIDED LAW: physical execution follows a forecast graph built from the workflow's dependencies + the live pool state (arXiv 2609.03335, latency-aware orchestration on heterogeneous pools):** the runtime's shape: logical workflow (what computation is required) vs physical schedule (placement, model-lifecycle, ordering chosen from observed pool state); predictor propagates device-specific estimates through workflow dependencies to forecast readiness; scheduler optimizes selection/placement/order on live state. Gains: makespan −36.8%, p95 −25.9%, GPU-seconds −24.63/session under burst arrivals. Translation: the scramble's two layers — the logical mission sets (the triage's packages) and the physical dispatch (which exact free seat, which runner, which fork, at which window, with which fallback) — should be separated exactly this way; the physical layer is chosen from LIVE pool state at dispatch time, never from a static roster. This is the field's formalization of the rig's "rode the live tip, not the tracking ref" discipline, at fleet scale.

**F5 — THE MULTI-OBJECTIVE LAW: the fleet's objective is not speed or cost alone — it is the probability of completing each package within its constraints (arXiv 2611.13740 multi-objective scheduling; MCPP's constraint-driven framing):** the 2026 cloud-scheduling canon: jointly minimize makespan and cost while maximizing utilization — and MCPP reframes it correctly for missions: **maximize the probability the entire workflow completes under explicit budget AND deadline constraints**, allocating models and parallel samples across simultaneously-executable subtasks while managing the remaining budget and time, then replanning after observations. Translation: the scramble's success signal is neither "fast" nor "cheap" — it is "the probe lands within its window within its budget, probability-maximized" — and the replan-after-each-landing IS the opus-of-opuses rule (the landings decide the next sprint-mission), now with the OR canon behind it.

## The refinement (the scramble's five laws, mapped)
1. **Name the prerequisite edges first** (F1): A's oracle-arm seat depends on B's supply survey → B dispatches first or A carries its own survey leg; the package ordering co-decides feasibility.
2. **Read seats as spot offers** (F2): the calendar's rows carry price/completion-probability/deadline per seat; fallback = the existing retry discipline.
3. **Allocate from trace history, don't precommit** (F3): the dispatcher keeps the fleet's per-seat success/429 history and allocates per dispatch (the fleet's CRO); the flagship replans from landings (the fleet's MCPP).
4. **Logical vs physical separation** (F4): the mission sets are logical; the exact seat/runners/forks are chosen from live pool state at dispatch — never a static roster.
5. **Probability-of-completion is the objective** (F5): each package's success bar = completes within window within budget, maximized; the replan-after-landing is the sequencing law.
