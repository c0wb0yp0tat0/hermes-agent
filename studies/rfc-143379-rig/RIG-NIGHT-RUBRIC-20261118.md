# RIG-NIGHT-RUBRIC-20261118 — the reader's checks, for the critique stages

The rubric is the bar the night report must clear. It is written for a reader, not a maintainer. Every item is a check a lane can run against the document and the exemplar (the earlier memo, whose opening sentences are quoted in the draft brief). A check fails when the document's own words fail it; the critic's verdict quotes the failing sentence.

1. **The listen check.** Read the document aloud in your head. Every sentence holds its breath where a listener breathes. No sentence stumbles or reads assembled. Failing lines are quoted and rewritten in the delta.
2. **The stranger check.** A person who never saw the rig or the study understands every sentence on the first read. Every noun is either plain or explained in its own sentence. No file names, no gist ids, no branch names, no internal titles used as if the reader knows them.
3. **The temperature check.** The prose sits comfortably beside the exemplar's register: calm, specific, plain, honest. Short sentences mixed with long ones. Numbers spelled in words with their meaning beside them. No exclamation, no sales, no drama. Cold, assembled sentences are quoted in the delta.
4. **The voice check.** The machine's row vocabulary is absent from the prose: no SUBJECT, STATE, RECEIPTS, verdict lines, seats, gates, cells, spines, comparators. No em dashes. No bold runs. No bullet lists. Plain paragraphs only.
5. **The facts check.** Every kept fact traces to the night's record (the sources named in the draft brief). What could not be confirmed is said honestly in a plain sentence or left out. No number appears without its meaning in the same sentence.
6. **The ask check.** The two questions close the document: the pilot question present exactly as quoted, with the recommendation to decide nothing yet; the daily digest present as one small envelope a day, five minutes, cancelable, recommended. The page ends by handing itself back to the reader, with the register line at the very end.

The gates of the run: the draft passes when a cold critic clears all six, and the final version passes when a second cold reader clears all six without having seen the critique. Any failure returns to the writer with quoted deltas, not general notes.
