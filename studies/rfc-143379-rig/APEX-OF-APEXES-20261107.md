## 4. THE READ-BURN SEAM — the 5-why's properly-stated problem

### 4.1 The problem (properly stated)

**The write-window is brief-enforced, NOT harness-enforced.** The write-hold (skeleton by round 8, FULL by round 20, git-only after round 20) exists only as prose in the brief-armament; the harness does not compel it. A leg can read indefinitely and still be "in budget" until the prose is honored.

### 4.2 The 5-why

1. **Why do legs read past their named ground?** Because the mission names ground ("read what the mission names") but the harness does not bound the reading — no tool-call budget, no read-phase deadline. The 1M-window rule is a *discipline*, not a meter.
2. **Why is there no meter?** Because the write-window's numbers (rounds 8/20) are round-counts a *driver* counts, not counters the harness increments per tool call. The harness sees calls, not rounds.
3. **Why does the driver count rounds instead of calls?** Because the brief-armament is written for a human-direction loop (the head folds, rows, re-fires) where round-talk is natural; the copycat harness's native units are tool calls and wall-clock, and the brief never translated.
4. **Why was the brief never translated?** Because every night the brief is inherited (the standing armament + the landing-verb + the substance-contract) and the inheritance is verbatim — the translation layer is assumed, never built. The read-burn is the seam the inheritance hides: legs optimize the *read* (rich, receipted, safe) over the *write* (compressed, exposed), because only the read is legible to the next leg.
5. **Why does the read stay legible and the write stay late?** Because the write is the leg's only public act (tracked file + remote HEAD == commit), and lateness costs nothing until the two-writer discipline collides (a rejected push, a rebase) — the failure is externalized to the *next* leg, not the leg that burned the reads. **The root: the write-window's enforcement lives in the brief, one layer above the harness that burns the reads, and nothing in the harness makes the late write fail.**

### 4.3 The countermeasure (named for the head's local-tooling pass)

**The harness write-deadline at a named round.** The head's local-tooling pass is to implement, in the copycat harness:

- **Skeleton-by-round-8, FULL-by-round-20, git-only-after-20** as *harness counters*, not brief prose: the harness counts the leg's tool calls converted to round-equivalents (or counts driver turns directly) and, at the round-20 mark, hard-disables every non-git tool (the terminal goes git-only: exactly two calls, the commit + the push).
- **The landing-gate stays as the harness's own post-condition**: artifact tracked + REMOTE HEAD == the leg's commit, checked by the harness after the git-only window — a leg that has not written by round 20 fails *in the harness*, not in prose.
- **The read-phase budget**: a named read-call ceiling per leg (named in the brief as a tool-call count, not a round count), so the 1M-window rule gains a meter at the layer that burns the reads.

Until that pass lands, the read-burn seam is logged here as the score's standing seam — corroborated by the night's own shape (the plan's five rows vs the handoff's six arms is a read-carry seam of the same family: the goal-table was carried forward without the sixth arm being re-pinned).
