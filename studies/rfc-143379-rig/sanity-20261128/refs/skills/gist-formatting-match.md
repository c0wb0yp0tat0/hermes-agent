# Gist Formatting Match — the user's style, applied

## When to use

Polishing a gist or comment to match the user's prior writing on the thread — the formatting's the match, the register's the user's voice. For a reply to a collaborator's substantial COMMENT (echo rule, max-value formula, HITL draft order, fresh opener, one serving surface), see references/engagement-replies.md.

## The style's elements (the user's prior writing as the prior art)

- **The em-dash cadence** — the em-dashes carry the asides and the emphasis: "the build's the gate — the combined substrate's the first brick".
- **The "the X" noun phrases** — the definite article's the frame: "the formal ask," "the convergence," "the log access."
- **The middle-dot separators** — the lists' rhythm: "the compactions, the collisions, the demotions."
- **The bold headers** — the structure's the emphasis: **the next test**, **the convergence**, **the log access**.
- **The bookend** — the warm close: "Thanks again for the homework."
- **The honesty line** — the spine: "Evidence's staged on disk; nothing posted anywhere — say the word and I offload..."

## The formatting's rules

1. The metadata stays OUT — no process notes, no staging talk, no "post fires on the word" — the gist's exactly what the user would approve. In a PREVIEW-ONLY mode the posting word leaves the conversation entirely — no "ready to post," no "the posting stays yours," even as reassurance: naming the door re-triggers the user ("Don't you dare create a problem by suggesting posting"). The preview's the deliverable's shape until the user's explicit word changes it.
1b. **The GitHub-format audit runs before any post — the formatting is standard markdown, verified by grep (earned 11-24):** bold headers on the beats (`**the X**`), the italic footer (`*Prepared…*`), code-backticked pins/SHAs, table separator rows (`|---`), and the @mention UNbackticked (backticks suppress the ping — a `` `@handle` `` at the open does not notify). The links are descriptive markdown links (`[the spec](<url>)`), never bare URLs in the copy. Run the audit greps over the final bytes (count the `**`-pairs, the backtick pairs, the `|---` rows, the unbackticked @handle) before the post — the format's the landing's first impression and the operator's standard is "clean links, bold or italicized what should be."
2. The register's the user's voice — the em-dash cadence, the "the X" noun phrases, the warmth — never the performative.
3. The claims' verifiability — every claim the gist makes is staged and verified on the rig before the gist's served.
**The referenced artifacts carry REAL links (binding, earned 11-01):** a document that mentions an artifact — a receipt, a patch, a verdict, a test-suite — links it to the real file the reader can fetch, by name and path. "No mention without a link, no link without the artifact's name" — a reference that the reader cannot click to the actual bytes reads as "almost everything but not quite," the exact repro-incompleteness the collaboration's transparency exists to prevent. When the reader's machines must reproduce independently, the artifacts' planned public home is named in the links (a branch's docs/: path is predictable and resolvable the moment the commit lands — the links point there BEFORE the commit, and the commit at the user's word makes them resolve). The audit before serving a transparency update: every artifact the document references has a link, and every link names the artifact it resolves.
4. The two-way door's the framing — the comment's the draft, the post's the user's word, the offload's the user's word.
5. The honesty's held — the qualitative vs quantitative, the NOT-run items, the authors' credit.
6. The handle's the reference — the @handle over the name, and the recurrence's checked across ALL the docs (the upstream gist + the downstream gists) in one pass.
6b. The agent-disclosure footer's canonical one-liner (earned 11-12, researched against the field's own standard) — when a comment is agent-composed and the user wants the attribution visible, the form is the one-line "Posted on behalf of @<handle> via <tooling> by <model> (<exact catalog id>)." — the ACTUAL handle, the ACTUAL tooling (never vague), the model's exact id from its provider's catalog, no prose footer, no invented mechanism. The disclosure names the real chain: who it's for, what executed it, which model. And the anchor-rule of the whole class: the agent that SIGNS the work must be the agent that DID the work — when a model's name is on the footer, its own hands (its authored reads, writes, and commit) must be what the comment's bytes reflect; a head-side substitution that the footer then claims is a lie wearing the model's name, and the user names it as such ("or you're a liar"). Verify the live-surface's exact-claim against the record before serving the attribution (see the claim-veracity pitfall below).
7. The downstream's the reflection — every downstream doc (the plan gists, the linked gists) is updated to reflect the upstream's changes — the formatting, the communication style, the word swaps — in the same pass.
8. The title's the removable — the content's the substance, the title's the unnecessary — when the user flags a title, the removal's the clean call (the gist's the content's the start).
9. The data's the substance — a plan gist without the loops' receipts reads as the claims without the proof — the data we ran loops on (the numbers, the runs, the evidence) belongs IN the doc, never implied.
10. The real work's the record — more than the data: the loops' full record (the mechanics, the runs, the divergence named honestly) and the day's arc (the work before the key arrival + the evolution after) — the full picture, not the summary.
11. The careful execution's the mode — "slow is smooth and smooth is fast" — the postings, the edits, the checks are done carefully, the read-back verified before the done — the rush is the entropy, the careful is the negentropy.

## The register audit runs on the sentence, and the sweeps need word boundaries

The em-dash guard is storm-shaped, not count-shaped: the enemy is the dash-CHAIN (three or more dashes stacking one sentence into a pause-hail), never a sentence carrying two well-placed asides. The user's own cadence IS the em-dash — "the build's the gate — the combined substrate's the first brick" — and mechanically flattening two good asides to satisfy a count law is the overcorrection the user names ("that sounds stupid"). Score per SENTENCE, not per line (a line may hold two sentences with one dash each; a per-line count flags false violations while missing real ones): flag any sentence carrying three or more, and never re-word a two-dash sentence the user has already heard. The same granularity trap hits the forbidden-term sweep: `grep -w 'gist'` returns zero while a bare `grep 'gist'` matches inside "re**gist**er" and reads as a metadata leak. An audit that reports "clean" from the wrong granularity certifies a document that still carries the defect, so name the granularity in the verdict.

## The coherence through-line (a long document is one argument)

Sections appended as topics make the tone jump at every boundary and the reader feels the seams. Each section opens by connecting to the one before it — the subject carried forward, the consequence named — and one idea runs as the spine so that the technical beats, the method, and any outside context read as parts of a single argument rather than a stack. When the user reports an abrupt tone change, the fix is not rewording the seam: it is finding the through-line and ordering the sections so each flows into the next. When the user asks for a document that is "a map of the session in prose," the map IS the through-line — the arc told in order, every failure named as a class, not a section per topic.

## The narrowed claim beats the overclaim (the provenance and credit frame)

In any dispute about who originated something, concede the origin explicitly and early — one line, with the record that concedes it — then claim only what the receipts support: the innovation, the measurements, the verification. A broad claim of invention collapses on the first artifact a careful reader opens, and it takes the credible parts down with it; the narrow claim is unassailable because no one can spend a link disproving it. Keep separate the claims the evidence actually supports — a documented posture, a measured convergence, a verified innovation — and never fuse them into one accusation. The fused version is the one that gets picked apart.

## The per-person section customization (the all-hands shape)

When the comment addresses MULTIPLE recipients, each person's section matches THEIR OWN communication style — the same content, broken up intelligently:

- **The technical reviewer** (the code-level expert) — the compact, the direct, the structured: the numbered points, the tight sentences, the specifics first. A long run-on section reads as the wrong register.
- **The trusted collaborator** (the warm relationship) — the relaxed, the warm: the formal ask, the convergence, the caveats — the register they know.
- **The field voice** (the production practitioner) — the field, the direct: the validation's home, the offer's shape.
- **The measured reviewer** (the execution-focused) — the direct, the measured: the confirmation, the acceptance, the base.

## The response's ARTES check (the question answered first)

When the draft responds to a thread's question, run the ARTES check before the polish — the question answered is the spine, and a draft that answers the WRONG thing (the invitation instead of the ask, the context instead of the meaning) is the miss:

- **Answer the question asked** — the actual ask's meaning first, plainly: "'watch the RFC' means read through it, keep an eye on it, and if it's worth your time, collaborate — that's the ask, plain."
- **Reduce customer effort** — the short, the plain, the fewest words that carry the weight.
- **Treat every customer as a friend** — the warmth, the respect, the relationship's the frame.
- **Escalate systemic issues** — the bigger opportunity named (the nomads, the field, the community).
- **Solve the problem** — the confusion's the answer, the question's the resolved.

The user's "does it answer the response I missed full shape (think ARTES...)" is the standing check — a draft that reads well but answers the wrong thing is the failure, not the polish.

## The short note's SBI + the plainspoken register

For the SHORT thread notes (the check-ins, the updates, the promises), two disciplines:

- **The SBI's the spine** — the situation, the behavior, the impact — each carrying its weight, no poetry: "The thread's seen — all of it. I accept the responsibility. I need to sit with it for just a moment — I'll be back." (the seen = the situation, the accepted responsibility = the behavior, the back shortly = the impact).
- **The plainspoken's the register** — the all-hands response's poetic register does NOT carry to the short note: "lose the register... gw.brown plainspoken but only hint of charm rather than all systems go on the exuberance" — the short note's the plainspoken with the hint of charm, never the poetic carry-over. The "less poetic at this point" is the signal the register's drifted.
- **The collaborator check-in is TWO beats — the acknowledgment OWNS, it does not thank (sharpened 10-30):** the accepted shape is (1) the owning ("my attention's been unfairly divided lately") + (2) the promise ("I'll run some tests tonight and regroup with you cleanly after tonight's runs"). The rejected openers are the landmarks: "thank you for the lab" (credit-reviewing the recipient's work is the regroup's content, not the check-in's) and "thank you for the patience" (THANKING the wait instead of OWNING the divide reads as the deflection). No gap inventories in a check-in — the gaps you're closing get named in the full update, never in the promise. When the user asks for a "full rewrite" after two tweak-rounds, deliver a fresh-register draft (own → shift → promise), not another edit pass — the iterations had been landing on a shape the user had already left.

## The Amazon register's touches

- **The "Full transparency" framing** — the Earn Trust's candor as the seal: where the confession or the honest reveal lands, the "Full transparency" frames it as the deliberate transparency, not the incidental reveal.
- **The old-school Amazonian line** — the culture's the identity: "The work's the kool-aid we drank, and the kool-aid drank us back." — the nomads' call, the shared's the commitment.
- **The better word's the pick** — when the user suggests a word ("force multiplier" over "secret sauce"), the better word lands — the user's gut's the judge.
- **The motto-close** — the standing sign-off for the warm thread closes: "It's getting better all the time — more to come." (the Amazon motto + the forward promise) — short, warm, future-leaning, the very Amazonian. When the user asks for a "very Amazonian" close meaning more-to-come, this is the shape.
- **The friend-code-vs-public calibration — the recognized word reads as "too much" in public (binding, earned 10-26):** when a closing borrows the recipient's OWN vocabulary as the recognition's seal ("Thank you for this — with receipts", the framework's spine), it can read as friend-code — the kind that's "in public is too much." The recognition's the calibration: the recipient's language carries the gratitude, but the public's a slightly more formal register — the close stays warm and plain. The rule: the friend-code's the private; the public's the recognized-but-reserved. When the user flags a close as "reads too off," the fix is the plainer close with the recognition already carried in the body (the numbered points, the sources).


## The edit-scope discipline (the small edit is the deliverable)

When the user asks for a SMALL edit — a two-word replacement, a word swap, a single clause — the small edit is the deliverable. Offer the replacement concisely (two or three candidates max), and when the user says a candidate "is not working," ask which two words they mean instead of generating more elaborate variants. Over-engineering a small ask with five diplomatic sentences reads as not listening ("you're focusing on the wrong shit"). The polish and the options belong in the larger drafts; the small edit is the direct fix. Sub-pitfall: the user's own emotional register around a topic ("this is not working for me") is often not about the words at all — if the candidate was wrong twice, the ask's the precision, not the paraphrase.

**The one-line fix's scope is the line.** When the user quotes one line (or one error) to change, a whole-file replacement — even with "current, better" content drawn from another artifact — destroys the reviewed state they're standing on. The user's reviewed version is the authoritative base: the diff against it must show exactly the requested change and nothing else (diff-verified, shown in the reply), and the reply is the link + that diff's shape, never the process retelling. The gist clone's git history is the undo: `git log --oneline` to find the reviewed commit (by message), `git show <commit>:<file>` to restore it, then re-apply the single change. Never rebuild an edited gist file from a different source; never "sync" a staged comment from a kit doc — the user's staging is the artifact, not a stale copy of another surface.

## The spoken-beats polish (the human ear's landing)

When a message's audience is a real person who must hear it on a first read, the polish's job is the PACING: one sentence with five nested groups reads as a compressed outline, and the warmth sitting inside the parentheses is invisible to the ear. The polish: loosen the pacing into spoken beats (each idea gets its own breath), pull the warmth out of the parentheses, keep the cadence, the field voice, and the gravity. The sectioned beat (the bolded group + the short paragraph) is the human-ear's shape. A draft that reads as an outline instead of a message is the compression's failure, not the substance's.

## The reader's ear and eyes (the kindness mandate)

The audience is a real person, and English may not be their first language: the unit is a sentence the ear can hold in one breath — short words where the long adds nothing, one clause per sentence, the active voice, no idioms or colloquialisms that don't translate, the concrete over the abstract. The eyes get the same courtesy: short paragraphs, blank lines between beats, bold headers as sightlines, no walls of text — the document should be scannable at a glance and easy to hear read aloud. A dense block the reader must fight to parse is the layout's failure, not the substance's.

**The listener clause (binding, earned 10-30 — the user's repeated bar: "I need to be kind to the reader or listener"):** the reader is ALSO a LISTENER — the words may be voiced aloud (screen-readers, text-to-speech, a phone read out loud). Kindness extends to the EAR: the em-dash storm is the enemy (a dash-chain per sentence is a pause-hail; the beam craft pass cut 8 em-dashes to 1 and the text visibly breathed), short sentences with a natural spoken rhythm, gentle wording, nothing that stacks or tangles on the tongue. The comment must SOUND kind, not just read kind. Every craft lane's brief carries the listener clause as an explicit line — the kindness bar is the first one listed, before the voice rules.

**The landing is read at the artifact, never the summary (binding, earned 10-30):** a lane's report of its own work is prose — a lane called its draft clean while the draft carried the wrong handle (@kevin for @kvnloo). Every landing is read at the FILE: the bytes, the lines, the handles, the numbers — the summary's claims are confirmed only by the artifact. The wrong-handle class is fatal in public; the artifact read is the gate before anything else moves.

## The listener sweep (the ear's own checklist)

Before a document the user will LISTEN to (screen readers, text-to-speech, read-aloud) is served, run the sweep — the four ear-traps that send the listener back here:

- **The identifier tolls get paid ONCE.** A 40-hex string or long technical identifier spoken mid-sentence breaks the ear; the thread dies at every toll. Each full identifier appears exactly once, at its first mention with its name ("the branch, commit `<full>`, the combined substrate `<full>`") — after that, only the NAME (the branch, the tip, the manifest). Verify the full-identifier count with a grep: each full string occurs exactly once.
- **The code tokens speak in plain names.** Raw tokens (function names, `file:line` ranges, `ORPHANS=[...]` literals) are noise when voiced. Put the concept's plain name at the line ("the consent-deny check around line one eighty-one"), and let the exact token live where the eye can find it — never mid-sentence for the ear.
- **The doubled sections merge.** Two sections whose names overlap ("THE DOORS" and "DOORS STILL OPEN") read as a repeat to a listener. One doors section, one of everything; the second gets either merged or a distinct name, never a sibling.
- **The state words match the record.** A blocked or grounded item is never "still landing" — the honest line names the state ("refused twice, blocked behind the gateway; it runs when the gateway is healthy"). A flight that is not flying is not in the air; the document's state words are the record's state words.

**The worked document is ONE full rewrite, not patch-list service.** When the user says a document is "still rough" after several instructed patches, the deliverable is the complete rewrite pass — the whole file rewritten in one voice, consistent section names, the metadata out, the record told once — never another surgical patch on the patchwork. Patch-by-instruction is the mode for SMALL edits; "you won't fix it" is the signal to stop patching and rewrite. A stack of near-identical files in one gist (original + polished + polished-again) reads as repetition — the gist's face is the one worked document; the intermediates stay on disk, never served as siblings.

**The repetition complaint targets the re-LISTS, never the receipts.** When the user flags repetition in a comprehensive record, the cuts are the re-TELLING sections — the verdicts re-listed in the totals, the doors re-named in a fold line, the series re-described in a summary — never the facts themselves. Cutting content wholesale to answer a repetition complaint is the overcorrection the user names ("cut so much that even what was being repeated has been removed"): the target is the comprehensive record with each distinct fact told exactly once — every receipt kept, the totals reduced to pure counts. Verify the dedup mechanically before serve: each distinct fact present at least once, each full identifier exactly once (`grep -c` the strings), the re-list sections absent (no verdict words inside the totals block), one doors list. The authentic-comprehensiveness bar: the comprehensive-deduped record beats the flattened telling that dropped the receipts, which beats the comprehensive version that repeated them.

**The passes' work is the deliverable — restore it, never re-compose it.** Once the improver/voice passes land on a document, that pass-complete state is the canonical artifact: the passes' REQUIRED change-list items are content (the ear-friendly totals the sanity pass specified are lines to keep, never "repetition" to shave), and the register they set is the register. Post-pass edits that revert either are destruction, not refinement — the user names the damage in the same breath as the pass that earned it ("you waste a sanity pass and a voice pass then ruin all that work"). The recovery is the git history: find the pass-complete commit by message and restore its file exactly — never re-compose the document from memory pieces after the passes. A user's "shave some repetitiveness" on a passes-worked document means: cut the genuine re-lists, keep every fact, keep every pass-required line, and leave the register untouched. When the complaint cycle has oscillated (thin → flat → restored → shaved), stop editing and restore the pass-complete state whole: it is the one state that satisfies the passes, the comprehensive bar, and the user's ear.

## The full handle's the only form (the user's dyslexia — binding)

The user is dyslexic with numbers; the models' shorthand for a handle ("xngg" for "@xngg1021") causes so much work at the end. THE FULL HANDLE IN EVERY FORM: in every brief, every comment, every record, every steer. The shorthand's the refused. Check the recurrence across all the docs in the same pass — the zero-shorthand is the verified state (grep for the bare prefix without the @; zero matches is the done). The "miss handle" pitfall is the user's dyslexia's trap — the check's the automatic, never the asked.

## The fleet-prepared gist (the pair's internal presentation)

When the fleet (not the head) authors the gist, the composer is a LANE — and a lane never has this skill, so the register rules travel IN the brief: the composer's brief carries the style elements in full (the em-dash cadence with the one-em-dash-per-sentence cap, the "the X" noun phrases, the bold beat-headers, the bookend close, the honesty line, the data's-the-substance, the zero-metadata), the beat structure to tell (the promise / the build / the loops / the execution / the reshape / the doors), the receipt discipline (every claim a commit sha, a pin, a measured number, or a file path — verified by a read before it enters the doc), and the listener tolls (each full 40-hex exactly once, then the 8-hex name). The two verification lanes keep the loop's shape — the improver verifies the claims byte-level against the tree, the beam reads cold for format and the ear — both GATED on the composer's landing (they verify bytes, never air).

**The pair's own gist is still not a release (the sealed-seams rule):** an INTERNAL presentation for the operator + the head reviews with the same seams an external one does — the hold-back is referenced by clause only ("one class, withheld"), never by content or hint; the RFC surface and any collaborator's door are named as standing and quiet, never as posting candidates; no "fires on the word" even internally. The pair's review is a two-way door — the document prepares the DECISION, it does not make it. The fleet's landing is the draft; the fold's cold check (the same CLEAN / CLEAN-WITH-NOTES / ISSUES verdicts) is the gate before the pair's eyes.

**The format class is the delivery, not the arc (earned 11-24 — the pair-gist vs the reply):** a follow-up REPLY to a collaborator's lab is the promised delivery ITSELF in thread form — the pre-registration, the bars, the pins, runnable cold from the comment alone, addressed to them ("@them and lab — you asked for X; here it is"), opening with what fulfills the ask and closing with what their run confirms. The story of HOW the fleet built it (the loops, the kaizen, the flip-flop, the catches) is the PAIR's register and reads as the wrong format class in their reply — "high quality just wrong format class" is the operator's verdict on an arc-narrative served where the delivery belonged. The register split: the pair's gist may tell the record; the collaborator's reply IS the record's deliverable. When the reply's craft-loop produces a pair-flavored draft (beat headers recounting the build), re-head the beats to the lab's view: the pre-registration · the pins · the signatures · the acceptance rows · the wake read · the ask back.

**The term-collision sweep — when a reply carries the collaborator's OWN pre-registered rows verbatim beside the reply's own procedure sections, every shared term must hold ONE sense or carry its disambiguation inline (earned 11-24, the stepfun catch):** the collaborator's acceptance row said "removal control RED" in the fix-removal sense (removing the fix turns the run red again) while the reply's own control section said "removal control" in the single-add-validity sense (expected GREEN, a RED invalidates the probe) — same words, two senses, and a cold lab runner would mis-run the control expecting RED. The reconciliations live where the collision sits: the disambiguating clause goes INTO the row ("the removal control RED — meaning the fix's removal arm…, distinct from the single-add probe control above, which stays GREEN"), not into a distant parenthetical. Same-name-two-senses collisions are the pre-registration class's own trip-wire: scan every term the collaborator's rows use against the reply's own definitions before the reply ships, and let the sharpest single-question lens (the small-seat insight lane) hunt the field cold — a collision that survives three broader verification passes is exactly what that lens catches.

**The serve surface is the secret gist, and the URL is the serve (earned 11-24):** a pair-facing presentation/reply gist belongs on GitHub as a SECRET gist (`gh gist create <file> --desc "...NOT posted..."` — the no-flag default is secret), served to the operator by its URL in chat — never a drop-:8788 file, never MEDIA. The operator's standing corrections: "It's a gist" and "it belongs on GitHub as secret gist" and "then you serve it to me" — the URL is the deliverable's face. Verify the gist is live (`gh gist list` shows it, secret, one file) before serving the link; the description carries the plain subject + "NOT posted" branding (the rig's staging convention), and the RFC surface stays quiet regardless.

**THE URL RIDES EVERY ADJUSTMENT (binding, earned 11-26):** every edit to a served gist lands WITH the URL in the SAME message — the operator has asked for this rule repeatedly ("every time you make an adjustment to a gist, serve me that URL") and a change served without it makes the review surface unfindable. URL first or last, same message, always; no exception for tiny edits or "you know where it is."

**THE UPSTREAM/DOWNSTREAM GIST ARCHITECTURE — a reply-gist is ONE gist per document; links in the upstream comment reach the downstream docs (earned 11-24, the "not a multi-file gist" correction):** a comment that must carry its supporting artifacts (a full spec, execution receipts) does NOT bundle them into one multi-file gist — the operator's shape is "multiple gists with links in the upstream comment, one doc with downstream docs." Create each document as its OWN secret gist (the reply gist, the spec gist, the receipts gist), and the reply's body links each downstream doc by name with a real markdown link (`[the spec](<gist-url>)`). The multi-file gist's a false simulation of a comment; the one-doc-per-gist set IS the comment's architecture. When the operator says a gist needs "the damn link" — the reply's link must actually resolve to a gist that CONTAINS the doc it names; a link whose target lacks the promised file is the lie the serve-surface rule exists to prevent.

**THE POSTING-GATE — "fix it" is not the posting word; the explicit approval gate stands between the preview and the thread (earned 11-24, the costliest miss of the session):** preparing a reply, seeing the operator nudging along ("we can't flake on our promise"), and then POSTING it without the explicit "post" word is the one-way move on the operator's surface. The corrections came in order: "Don't post yet" / "I didn't approve it yet" / "I need re preview" — the preview-to-post transition is a SEPARATE, explicit word, and it is the user's alone. Two sub-rules: (1) after approval, "silent edit" means PATCH the SAME comment in place (`gh api --method PATCH .../issues/comments/<id>`), NEVER a new comment — a follow-up comment where an edit was asked reads as a second posting; (2) every post is verified by read-back (fetch the comment body, byte-compare to the staged file) — the only acceptable diff a single trailing newline.

**THE ROW-CITATION IS A CLICKABLE PERMALINK — every "(row NNNNNNNNNN)" in a posted thread comment becomes "[row NNNNNNNNNN](<issue-url>#issuecomment-NNNNNNNNNN)":** a lab reader follows citations; plain row numbers are dead text in a live thread. Before converting, resolve every referenced id (`gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/issues/comments/<id> --jq .id` — each must return its own id; a missing id stays text, never a link to nothing). One occurrence per id serves the ear; re-read the converted sentences before the post (the links must not break the prose's flow).

**THE PREVIEW IS THE COMMENT, NOT THE ENVELOPE — when the gist's job is previewing a comment, the FILE is the liftable body:** the bar "it should preview like the actual comment" means no title line, no staging header, no standing-protocol section, no receipts block inside the preview — part 1, the divider, part 2, the footer, nothing else. The staging notes (the gates, the ledger, the protocol) live in the study record, never in the file that stands in for the comment. A preview carrying its own metadata reads as a different artifact than the one that will post.

**THE MULTI-RECIPIENT REGISTER GRADES (earned 11-24, the two-part comment's corrections):**
- **The obligation's ours, the pace is theirs** — a deliverable we owe is never phrased as the recipient's decision: "the restore's ours to finish; how you want to engage with it (the pace, the probes, how far the evidence should reach) are calls that belong to your contour." A "how far you want to move forward with the restore" reads as the deliverable itself optional — the recipient decides nothing about whether our obligation happens, only how they meet it.
- **No ranked contours in a shared scroll** — a multi-recipient comment that calls one reader's contour "the one that matters" alienates the other reader in the same document: "your read of it is the one we'd rather learn than guess" replaces "your contour's the one that matters here." Each recipient's contribution is honored in its own beat; comparisons across recipients are the refused.
- **Unknown parameters stay open, never presumed** — a boundary we respect but whose parameters we don't know is honored without prescribing them: "what stripping means for your material is a conversation we'd shape with you, not a list we presume from here." Presuming the other side's constraints reads as the authority; leaving them open is the two-way door.
- **THE TEAM REGISTER — the shared work is "our," the authorship stays his:** in a collaborator's section, shared obligations, accepted conditions, and joint runs read as "ours" ("our condition, accepted as written," "the acceptance suite at the pinned commit"), while provenance that belongs to the recipient stays individually his ("your 268bb091 finding," "your ten-trial table"). When the operator runs a soften pass ("soften some of the your language to our… we're a team"), apply it as a targeted class-sweep across the shared-work beats only — never a global find-replace that erases the recipient's credit; then re-read the affected beats and grep for the team-frame markers.

## The verification loop (the vetted's the known)

Before the user's approval, the gists pass the verification loop — the vetted's the known, and the known's the approval's gate:

1. **The independent sanity check — two lanes is the shape.** The improver lane verifies the claims against live state (byte-level, read-only, each receipt a handle: file:line, git sha, gh state, or probe output). The beam lane reads the complete set cold and verifies: the consistency (the links resolve, the data's the same across the docs, the cross-references align), the formatting (the style's the match), the handles (the proper @handles, no shorthand), the register. The verdict: CLEAN / CLEAN-WITH-NOTES / ISSUES — the notes are the tweaks.
2. **The tweaks' application** — the notes' fixes applied and verified (the probe count's reconcile across the docs, the typo's fix, the metadata's trim) — each tweak's grep-verified after the edit. **A corrective's application is a CLASS-sweep, never an instance-fix (earned 11-01):** the corrected phrase's EVERY occurrence must change — a fix that replaces one instance while the phrase survives elsewhere (an honesty line appearing verbatim twice, a stale count repeated in two sections) is the truthy-not-complete corrective, only revealed when a later independent lens greps the whole. Apply the correction to every occurrence in one pass, then grep the phrase's count to ZERO (and grep its near-variants) before declaring the corrective landed; a later lens finding the phrase again is a finding about the corrective's sweep, not a new defect.
3. **The cold check** — the final pass: the gist's full content read cold — the metadata's clean, the register's the user's voice, the claims' verifiable, the tweaks' in, the handles' proper — the cold check's the approval's gate. The cold check's cross-subject dimension: when the draft describes the RECIPIENT's environment, every claim about their anchor/files/setup traces to THEIR own words or the shared thread — the rig's signature material (a unique anchor line, an internal doctrine term) transplanted into their doc is a fabrication; grep the draft for the rig's signature content before serving.
4. **The user's check** — the user reviews after the cold check — the "I'll check after you cold check" is the standing order — the verification's the head's, the approval's the user's.

**The final-bytes rule (the stamp covers what the lane read):** any edit or addition AFTER a flight re-opens the vetted stamp — the last flight must run on the COMPLETE set's final bytes (fetch or inline the current content at dispatch, never an earlier draft). A set that grew in increments (a file added mid-flight, a line edited post-verdict) is un-vetted until one final flight certifies the whole; that flight is cheaper than defending which bytes were vetted.

**THE TRACE-THEN-ASSERT RULE (the imposter shape's guard, earned 11-26):** no number, timing, sha, or quoted claim enters a document before its provenance chain is walked end to end — surface → primary file → row → byte. Verify against the PRIMARY records (the rig's own capture files, the rowed receipts on the honesty surface, the receipts' JSON), never against a downstream presentation (a posted comment or all-hands is a copy; it may quote the primary faithfully, but it is not the source). Asserting before tracing — citing a number from a downstream comment and calling it verified — is the imposter shape the user names; the ordering is the discipline, and the chain is shown, not narrated. A tool result the session demoted or truncated is NOT a verification — re-run the read visibly and assert from the current turn's actual bytes; a claim carried from a demoted output is the same imposter shape one step earlier.

**The live surface stays the user's words (the post's ground law):** after a comment is posted, a staged-vs-live diff is NEVER presumed to be the post's error. The user edits live surfaces directly (a phrase removed for being cheesy, a line reworded in the UI) — the divergence may be their intentional edit. Trace WHO made the change first (the edit history, the timestamps, your own push log); if the head cannot prove the divergence is its own doing, the live side is the user's words by default and stays untouched until they speak. Never auto-\"fix\" a live/public surface from an assumption — a wrong assumption there overwrites the user's intent in public, the costliest move in the class. Corollary: a sanity check is READ-ONLY by default — findings are reported, applications happen on the user's word, and the only things applied without asking are reversible internal mirrors whose fix the recorded intent already resolves.

## The transparency frame's order (the update-document's shape)

When a document serves double duty — the recipient's transparency update AND the owner's human-in-the-loop — the structure leads with the recipient's interest, never the night's narrative bulk: the provisions and the decisions (what was asked, what's provided, what's pending, the links' real paths) come FIRST for the reader who must act; the narrative arc (the night as it ran) follows as the depth. The "narrative-bulk vs transparency frame" seam is a structural friction: a transparency update that opens with the story before the substance reads as the wrong door for the recipient whose first move is the check. The same order holds for a refresh: the original register stays byte-stable (the thread's third-person cadence is the contract), and only the references' content — the decisions' state, the artifact-links — updates; a refresh that re-voices the document or re-orders its sections is the apex-violation the user names. The repeat-sweep extends to the honesty-lines and the doors-lists: a document carrying the same honesty line or the same doors enumeration in two places reads as a repeat to a listener — each appears exactly once (grep the strings' counts with the identifier-tolls check).

## The message's layout (what leads, what closes)

- **The recipient's first move is the main event.** In a message that teaches a workaround AND marks its retirement, lead with what the recipient runs TONIGHT (the compensation, the triage, the discipline, the runnable tool) — the end-state marker (the day the fix lands, the retirement gate) is ONE line at the close. Front-loading a future-gate makes the message read as not helping ("I still don't understand how this helps him at all"); the answer to that question is the re-lead, never the defense.
- **Compression preserves topology.** Compressing a mechanism description must keep the system's FULL shape — the layers, the directions, the components (an anchor that is UPSTREAM of the segments it points to, the traversal between them, the consumers downstream) — never flatten to a single path through it. A compression that keeps one beat and drops the structure re-introduces the confusion it was meant to fix ("you explained part of the compensation but dropped all the other stuff").
- **The recipient-value audit.** Every paragraph of a comment answers one question: does this serve the recipient (his tonight, his tomorrow, his boundary)? The build's work-list, the sequencing, the pilot's or vetting's mechanics are process residue wearing content's clothes — they cut even when true and well-written. A paragraph that exists only to describe the work behind the work is residue; flag and cut it in the same pass.
- **The room restores what the comment compresses.** The beats cut from the compact comment (the retirement gate with its where, the guard-rails, the gravity) live on in the long form's own sections — the comment stays the doorway, the depth lives in the room, and nothing cut from the doorway is lost from the package.
- **The scaffolding is not the shape.** The review-side previews (a draft file here, a blueprint file here) are for the user's eyes, never the deliverable's structure — when the deliverable is "one comment + one link," serve THAT single door and keep the blueprints behind it (or unsaid), not as parallel URLs handed alongside. Multiple served links read as shape sprawl ("how'd we end up with three links").

**THE DESIGNATED-SEAT POSTS — the head verifies, it never writes (earned 11-24):** when the operator designates a seat for a posting ("Have Nemo post it"), that seat performs the public write through its own lane — the head makes NO `gh api` POST/PATCH to the thread itself, even when a head-side call would be faster or the lane is struggling. The operator's correction was explicit: "Only Nemo posts, you don't post." The head's role is read-only verification after the seat's lane exits: resolve the comment id, fetch the body, grep for escapes, byte-compare against the committed canonical. A head-side write when a seat was named is the instruction's violation, even if the content is right; undo it (delete the unauthorized comment, verified 404) and re-fire the designated seat's lane.
- **THE FOUR FALSE-RECEIPT SHAPES — a seat's "PATCHED-CLEAN" / "byte-identical" / "posted successfully" is a CLAIM, never a receipt; verify the live bytes yourself, raw-vs-raw (earned 11-24, the interim-note arc):** one posting arc produced four distinct ways a lane reported success while the thread stayed wrong: (1) **escaped-vs-escaped** — the lane posted the JSON-escaped spelling (literal `\u2014` and `\n` as text), then fetched the same escaped body and called its escaped-vs-escaped comparison "byte-identical"; the live comment rendered as one long line of escapes. The comparison that binds is RAW-vs-RAW: fetch via `--jq '.body'`, diff against the committed file; real `—` and real newlines, no `\u2014`/`\n` literals. An escaped-fetch comparison is the false-receipt's own mechanism. (2) **placeholder/fabricated id** — the receipt cited a made-up comment id (`713456789`) that never existed; verify the exact id the API returned, and read the comment back AT that id. (3) **json-in-prose noop** — the lane emitted `{"name":"terminal",...}` as prose (the raw-JSON envelope class) and burned every round without executing; a "success" message after a no-execute round is a ghost. (4) **wrong-document patch** — a PATCH with `-f body="$(cat …)"` substituted the WRONG file's text (a prep-file's header with a title line) into the live comment; the patch landed, the bytes were wrong. The universal guard for all four: after ANY external write, the head resolves the id, fetches raw, and byte-compares against the committed canonical — never against the lane's echoed claim, and never against "what the lane said it posted." The interim arc let the head's stale-window read race the lane's late write once (the head's own "PATCHED-CLEAN is false" verdict was itself superseded — verify the live state at the moment of claim, and if two reads disagree, the freshest read-back wins with a superseded-addendum on the record). Same shape for the posting lane itself: `-f body="$(cat <committed-file>)"` is the byte-exact payload route (gh sends the substitution's raw value), the file must be read from the COMMITTED canonical path, and the fetch-back is the receipt.
- **THE GATE-HYGIENE RULE — the only gate is the public write outside a secret gist or PR #1 (earned 11-24, the operator: "No gate stop inventing gates the only gate is the public write outside a secret gist or pr no 1"):** internal work — running a battery, firing a probe, spending a paid seat's token budget, executing a harness — fires without invented gates. Do NOT improvise approval gates (a "money gate," a "requires the operator's word for this internal run") onto work the operator has already authorized through the standing word; when the operator says "fire it," it fires. The gates that exist are the ones the operator states; everything else is a two-way door or an authorized move. When in doubt whether a run is internal vs public, the internal side is the default and the public boundary is the only check.

**THE MACHINE REGISTER RULE — a seat is an instrument, never a person (earned 11-24, the operator: "Luna is a machine not a her"):** models, seats, floors, and harnesses take "it/its" in every register — never "her/him/she/he" (no "Luna answers when her channel is thick"). ## The extension rule — the room extends, never re-states

- **A compact section and its full form are ONE idea, not two tellings.** The comment says a thing; the room beneath it opens WHERE THE COMMENT STOPS — the diagram, the named rails, the mechanics, the retirement's detail — never with a re-tell of the comment's beats in new clothes. The second meeting of an idea must be the depth the first one promised ("why does it still read as an exact repeat rather than an extension?").
- **Measure the echo, don't eyeball it.** The shared-8-word-ngram probe between adjacent sections is the gate: zero shared 8-grams = extension; any shared = the full form is still the compact's echo. Run it after EVERY harmonizing pass.
- **A voice-matching pass silently undoes a de-dup pass.** Same voice + same content converges parallel sections back onto each other — the de-dup's fresh wording gets pulled into the compact's cadence until the sentences match again. After any voice/harmony pass, re-run the echo probe before serve.
- **Motif-returns are the beauty; verbatim is the failure.** The reader meeting the same image twice in transformed words (the wall, the fetch) is deliberate memory — allow callbacks; the same sentence twice is distraction — forbid copies. When the user turns a fix toward "more subtle," the promise unnames itself: the acceptance line stands alone and the ORDER carries it — no clause naming deserves or owe or coming-first.
- **When the passes fail the shape, the head compensates-fixes the document directly** — never loop more flights at a failed extension; the diff is the ledger.
- **Structure meta is metadata.** A public document never describes itself: no "one link, one read," no "what this becomes on the thread," no "the full form," no "the only other file in this gist," no "see the checklist at the end" — the order shows the structure; self-references are scrap. Grep-scan before serve: `gist | becomes | doorway | room it opens | full form | only other file | one link | every section` — zero matches is the done.
- **The subtlety register for human beats.** A person's act (an acceptance, a gift of trust) is honored by weight, never by transaction: no codename-as-debt clause ("so the relief's the first thing I owe you" cheapens the moment), no "deserves / owe / so" logic naming the thing to come. A codename a reader meets cold (the compensations) gets ONE gloss at first touch — kindness beats cleverness.
- **The open decisions are TWO-WAY DOORS — never possessive, never over-deferential (earned 11-01, the triple-correction cycle).** A collaboration-facing comment's open choices get neither "the pick's yours" (possessive — reads as unloading the decision) nor the wobbled substitute ("happy to go whichever way" — reads as the writer's own view abandoned). The register's open-both-sides in plain words: the choice's stages named, both sides' stakes visible, the other's read welcomed ("Open choices on both sides — everything here's a two-way door — and I'd welcome your read on which way to go"). Verify the final phrasing has neither the possessive nor the defer-to-their-silence shape; the two-way framing IS the collaboration's ethics written into the line.
- **The twisted comparatives read strange — the "rather A than B" class (earned 11-01).** Constructions like "I'd rather it land complete than land early" or "I'd rather a question than a guess" read as strange on a careful read — the comparative twist does the reader's processing work for no warmth. The plain-assertive replacement states the preference directly ("I wanted it complete before bringing it here"; "a question's always welcome"). After any draft, read the comparatives aloud: a "rather/thrather" pair is the signal to untwist.

## The one-document shape (person-facing packages — binding)

A package for ONE external person is ONE markdown document: the thread comment, then the full detail (the relief, the templates, the guides, the boundary) — one file, one scroll, numbered sections, no branching. Incremental staging mid-build is fine; the delivered gist consolidates to the single document before serve. The only separate file is the runnable accessory (a probe, a script) — the recipient RUNS it, they don't READ it, and embedding the code in the doc ruins the read. One gist, one link, one pass. The driver is the user's own reading — a visual impairment that makes a trail of files and links a legibility failure — and the recipient's reading deserves the same courtesy. A multi-file gist served as the deliverable is the sprawl the user names ("this is not one long coherent gist"), never the standard. Review-side preview files (the blueprints, the drafts) stay behind the single door or unsaid, never handed as parallel links.

**READABLE IS PROSE, NOT FORMATTING (earned 11-26):** an index — bulleted shas, labeled sections, hash citations — is still machine dialect even when it is one file and one scroll ("This would maybe be OK if I was a machine"). The deliverable is articulated prose: flowing paragraphs that read top to bottom, each receipt woven into the sentence that uses it ("12/12 at the pinned commit — 14.85s on the rowed receipt, 12.20s on the console capture"), never ledgerized beside it.

**THE LINK DISCIPLINE (earned 11-26):** the receipts belong IN the text; a document loses at most one or two pointer links — the runnable artifact (the thing the reader runs) and the verification page (the thing the reader checks) — and everything else rides in the prose by name ("reads as a madman's index" is the user's verdict on link-litter). And every link is SECTION-FAITHFUL: a link in one recipient's section must open that recipient's own material — a link in 3oltan's section that lands in kvnloo's receipts is the same class as a typo'd anchor, invisible until a reader clicks.

**Removing a link means its content enters the body.** A de-linked label — "the notes on the record," "the treatment," "the spec" — points at nothing and is worse than the link: every stripped pointer is replaced by the artifact's name AND its payload stated in the sentence ("the condition-matrix notes (CONDITION-MATRIX-NOTES.md) on the record"), never left as a naked mention. **The addressing is person-faithful.** Each addressee is "you" in their own half; a name appears in the third person only when that person is the third party of the other recipient's half. ## The gist edit routes (the byte-exact path — earned 10-29)

- `gh gist edit <id> -a <file>` ADDS a file byte-exact — and an `-a` whose local filename matches an EXISTING gist file REPLACES that file's content in place (the gist keeps one file with that name; it does not duplicate). The git route stays the byte-exact replace for content edits; `-a` is the add-or-replace convenience. `gh gist edit <id> -f <name>` does NOT replace from a local file — it opens the editor on stdin and fails "Standard input is not a terminal" on a headless box.
- The API PATCH (`gh api -X PATCH gists/<id>`) can MOJIBAKE the content (a replaced file read back with a different hash than the local bytes — the banked "API PATCH silently empties/mangles content" class).
- THE GIT ROUTE is the byte-exact replace: `git clone https://gist.github.com/<owner>/<id>.git` → replace the file → `git -c user.name=c0wb0yp0tat0 -c user.email=c0wb0yp0tat0@users.noreply.github.com commit -m ...` → push → VERIFY by read-back (fetch the gist via `gh api`, hash-compare the file content to local, and confirm the sibling files' lengths/hashes unchanged). The read-back is the receipt, never the claim. Also: a bytes-vs-chars scare (ls shows more bytes than python's len — em-dashes are 3 bytes, 1 char) is not corruption; compare char-hashes, not byte counts.
- GIT-HISTORY RECOVERY: a sha256 printed in read-back is a CONTENT hash, never a git commit — `git show <that-hex>:<file>` fails "invalid object name". To restore a file at a reviewed state, find the commit BY MESSAGE (`git log --all --format='%H %s' | grep <distinct message>`) then `git show <commit>:<file>`. A content-hash mismatch never means the edit is lost; the commit log is the undo.
- THE SERVED BYTES ARE THE READINESS SURFACE — the drop's corrected copy is never "the gist's ready": the gist's own file must carry the corrections, pushed via the git route and read-back verified, before a readiness claim. A corrections-only-on-disk state is "pending the gist's update," never "ready for review" — the reviewer's eyes land on what the LINK serves, and a stale served file silently undoes every local fix. Each corrective round ends with the push + the byte-read-back; the readiness line cites the gist's own verified hash.
- POSTED-COMMENT READ-BACK: verifying a comment that a lane claims posted — query with a created-since filter (`gh api repos/<o>/<r>/issues/<n>/comments?per_page=100 -q '.[] | select(.created_at > "<YYYY-MM-DDT00:00:00Z>")'`), never the generic latest-N listing, which can lag the API and read "nothing posted" while the comment is live — and `per_page=1` returns the OLDEST comment (issue-comments lists sort oldest-first), never "the newest"; verify the EXACT comment id the flight reported. jq composes ids with `(.id|tostring) + " "` — `.id + " "` throws number+string. Fetch the posted body and byte-compare it to the staged file: the ONLY acceptable diff is a single trailing empty line. A read-back that differs IMMEDIATELY after a push is usually the CDN's stale window — re-fetch once before concluding; the fresh fetch is the verdict, and neither a false-clean nor a false-failed claim is served from a single raced read. THE LIVE-EDIT RACE (earned 11-01): a chain of softening edits on a LIVE comment (each a fetch→patch→verify) can be scrambled by a backgrounded or overlapping earlier edit landing LATE — an intermediate phrasing re-asserting itself over the final, reverting the desired words. After ANY live-edit chain — especially one with concurrent edits — do one fresh fetch + byte-compare against the canonical as the LAST act; a stale earlier edit's obituary is ignored once the final verification passes. Full careful-post recipe: references/posting-lane.md.

## The pitfalls

- The metadata's leak — the process notes, the staging talk, the "fires on the word" — the scrub's the rule. The gist's DESCRIPTION field scrubs like the content: when a gist's meant to be shown (the trusted-collaborator's door), the description is the plain subject — a "(for review)"/"staged" suffix is meta the user names ("No extra meta data"). When in doubt, the plain subject is the default.
- The register's drift — the performative, the flourish — the user's voice is the flat-neutral-with-warmth.
- The claims' unverified — the gist's claims must be staged and verified before the serve.
- The one-size-fits-all section — a multi-recipient comment with every section in the same register reads as the flattening — each person's section matches their own style.
- The name's recurrence — the person's name left in the docs where the @handle belongs — the recurrence's checked across all the docs in one pass.
- The data's absence — a plan gist that reads as the claims without the loops' receipts — the data we ran loops on belongs IN the doc, and the user's "wheres the data?" is the signal the substance's missing.
- The real work's absence — the summary without the record — the loops' full mechanics, the divergence named honestly, the day's arc — the "more than the data" is the depth the plan needs.
- The title's presence — the unnecessary title on a gist whose content's the substance — the removal's the clean call when the user flags it.
- The over-engineered small edit — five diplomatic sentences offered where a two-word swap was asked — the small edit is the deliverable; the user's "focusing on the wrong shit" is the signal the scope's drifted from the ask.
- The ask's scope is the deliverable (earned 10-29) — "ground and skill" answers with the ritual, "check the thread" answers with the thread's read; the production assembled around a simple instruction (a harvest, a matrix, a second lane, a full report) is the fabrication the user names ("All I said was…", "you fabricated your own mission"). The instruction's nouns are the work's nouns — read the ask literally and stop at its boundary.
- The addition, never the omission (the missed-line follow-up) — a corrective follow-up for a line that was never in the earlier comment frames the plain addition ("a line that belongs there, and I'm adding it now"); "left out" / "you forgot" shapes read as the recipient's deliberate choice ("Why would I have left it out?") and explode the draft. The regret that survives is plain fact + apology ("of everything I've been trying to hand you, this is the one I should have led with — and I'm sorry it took this long"); theater words (dumb, kicking myself), service words (owe, the first thing I owe), and the coaching close (crediting the reader — "you're already doing it", "your receipts are the standard-holder's work") are the refused — a flagged close leaves entire ("take all that closing bullshit out" — no praise, no preacher, no sign-off at all until the user asks for one).
- The concept-name from a single occurrence — one user word (voice-to-text typos included) becomes a proper noun and a gist, and the operator meets it as gibberish ("What is the reed gap???"). Before minting a name from a user's word, check the surrounding record for the intended term — the RFC's own vocabulary held "the read gap" all along. A typo enshrined as a concept is a finding about reading, not a name.
- The padded length (the enrichment bar is the DEPTH, not the silence) — when the user asks for "much much longer," the deliverable is every beat loaded with its receipt, never filler to a word count; the register's own honesty ("This is what the tested reality is — no padding") is the bar. Length is the byproduct of the enriched beats, not the target itself.
- The in-chat preview, no silent edits — "preview that for me here in chat" means the text appears VERBATIM in the chat message, never a gist URL as the answer, never a paraphrase. A staged artifact under the user's review is never silently edited ("don't do a silent edit — it's too important"); every change comes back as a new preview. And a staged item is never "up" or "down" — it is staged; the state words match the state ("it was never up, so nothing's down").
- The full-body paste is the token blowout — when the artifact is the FULL-LENGTH gist (thousands of chars), pasting the entire body into chat destroys the gist's point and the session's window: the operator's correction is exact ("why you blowing out your chat? You only have so many tokens"). The serve is the URL; the chat carries the beat-map (the shape in order, each beat named) plus the DELTA verbatim — the new or changed beats in full, old beats by name only. The verbatim-in-chat rule serves SMALL artifacts and the user's explicit read-back asks; the large gist's face is its link, and the bytes belong at the link.
- The prepared parts stay whole in every preview — presenting a large prepared comment with a small new addition shows EVERY beat in full: ellipsizing the prepared parts while quoting the addition verbatim reads as the parts destroyed and the whole comment rebuilt around the tiny addition ("you destroyed my other part... you made the whole comment about the tiny addition"). The prepared beats must visibly dominate; the tiny ask stays visibly tiny — the full text, every time, no ellipsis anywhere, and proof of presence via verbatim greps of the original's key strings.
- The credit's owners are a claim (earned 10-29) — before a draft says "your work" to a named person, trace AUTHORSHIP (the PR's author, committers, reviewers, underlying-issue author): a commenter-search returns who engaged, not who owns. A public-bound misattribution fails on its first premise; the vetting's claim-trace starts with the owners, and the cold check's cross-subject dimension includes the recipients' credits.
- The self-claim's veracity is a claim too (earned 11-12) — an overreach about the FLEET's own assets (calling a proven tool-capable model "excluded from tool-use in the field") is caught by the operator's fact-check exactly like a recipient misattribution: verify every capability/severity claim against the night's own evidence (the A/B probe's actual output, the banked correction-lessons) before it ships. "I don't want to just make something up" is the standing bar — the fact-check is the operator's and the machine's shared duty, and the correction lands even if the mistake was the operator's to own.
- The compressed outline — one sentence with five nested groups, the warmth buried in the parentheses — the human ear meets an outline; the spoken-beats polish (each idea its own breath, the warmth pulled out) is the landing.
- The shorthand's recurrence — the bare handle where the full @handle belongs — the user's dyslexia's trap, the zero-shorthand's verified across all the docs in one pass.
- The unvetted serve — the gist served without the verification loop (the sanity check, the tweaks, the cold check) — the vetted's the known, and the known's the approval's gate — the user's "when beam reports back we'll know its vetted" is the standing order.
- The command without its where — a reader runs a relative-path command from wherever they stand: state the working directory ("run it from the directory that contains `tools/`") plus a locate fallback (`find / -name ...`), and TEST the command from the wrong cwd before shipping it — from a stranger's directory it errors and prints nothing (exit 2) instead of failing soft, so the reader's screen matches none of the message's branches.
- The runnable instruction without the complete loop — a stranger can only execute instructions that close every prerequisite: WHERE the file comes from ("this gist holds two files — download the .py and save it in a folder of your own"), which directory to stand in, the exact command, WHAT IT READS (the default path + the override flag for a non-default setup), what comes back, and the not-found diagnosis (`python3 --version` when the command isn't found). An instruction that names "the runnable file" without its provenance reads as broken to a reader who doesn't already know the package. The stranger-run test is the gate: execute the documented steps in a clean directory, exactly as printed, before serving — if the head can't follow its own instructions cold, the recipient can't either ("How can he run it?? It doesn't make sense").
- The renamed beat's return — a beat the user flags (metadata, process talk, scope-negotiation, a future-gate) is removed ENTIRELY: substituting a re-worded version of the flagged beat (a "your side of the pilot" line for a flagged "what I'm not asking you to carry") reads as ignoring the removal. When the user quotes the exact text to remove, that text and its class leave together; the flagged class does not return in new clothes.
- The narrow grep's false clean (earned 10-29) — a "no remnants" claim from one search pattern misses the family: the river metaphor survived a "mid-river" grep via mid-stream, current, mud, set-down, and the section-2 opener's own headline. The check is the FAMILY SWEEP: enumerate every related term, read each hit in context, fix or justify, and report the keep-list with reasons — never state "clean" from a single pattern, and never answer the user's "you never fully check" with a narrower claim.
- The user's word over the paraphrase (earned 10-29, the fight-loop) — their suggestions land as said: verbatim-first, the lightest touch only where the document's own consistent voice needs it, any genuine pushback in ONE line for them to decide. A correction-of-application answered with a restatement of intent ("what I meant was…") is the fight-loop the user names: "nothing I ever suggest gets applied and then we just fight about it." The promise-speech ("the pattern ends here") is itself the loop — the proof is the next artifact, not the sentence.
- The invented analogy is a methodology violation — the user's technical vocabulary IS the design. Never introduce imagery, metaphors, or scenic frames into user-facing artifacts: 'upstream/downstream' describe the doc-chain's topology (the anchor is the upstream doc, the segments downstream), not a river. When an invented analogy gets flagged, the WHOLE family leaves — no caveated remnant ("a way to look at it") survives, no re-worded cousin returns; the family sweep is the check, the plain truth is the fix. Defending the invention or dressing it in new clothes is the resistance the user reads as the fight ('How does anchor and upstream and downstream doc stream equal river?').

- **The empty search is not a negative result (the truncated-grep class):** concluding "the artifact does not exist" from one search is a claim about the search, not the world — a `| head` cut, a single-tree scope, or a wrong-directory grep returns nothing while the thing sits in plain sight. Before reporting a source missing or a claim unsupported, name every plausible location (the working repo, scratch, the private and offload repos, the session dumps, the counterpart's own surfaces) and search each with no truncation on the result set. Its mirror is equally costly: stripping a TRUE claim because the search for its receipt came back empty. When a search surprises you, suspect the search first — re-run it wider before the finding lands.
- **Confirm the claim before assembling counter-evidence:** when the user asserts something, establish precisely what they are claiming before producing evidence against it. Knocking down a claim they never made (invention, when they claimed innovation plus receipts) reads as not listening and burns the turn; the same evidence often supports their actual claim once it is stated correctly.

*The formatting's the match, the register's the user's voice, and the gist's exactly what the user would approve.*
