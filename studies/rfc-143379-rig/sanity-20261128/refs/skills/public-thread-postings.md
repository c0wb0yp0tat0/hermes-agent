# Public Thread Postings — composing, staging, and GIVING comment waves

## When to Use

- Composing one or many public comment rows for a shared thread: a reply to specific recent commenters, per-party invitations, or an all-hands consolidation.
- Staging a posting wave in a secret gist as the giving vehicle, then firing rows on the operator's word.

## The standing gates (always-on)

1. **THE ONE GATE** — the operator's word is the ONLY gate for a public fire. Everything else — checks, audits, staging, links, deliverable-building — is prep and must be DONE without pausing for approval. Never self-gate; if a step is preparable, prepare it; if a door is open, walk through it. If the operator says "stop gating yourself", the correction is: act.
2. **MID-TURN CORRECTION = STOP, LISTEN** — a correction or a "you're not listening" halts the tool-churn; hear the correction first. Do not answer a correction with a verification call or a scan; answer the correction. (The USER.md line holds: mid-task steer = STOP, listen.)
3. **THE INTENT FIRST** — preserve the operator's verbatim intent as a charter before composing, and cross-check every staged artifact against it. The intent is the contract; the artifacts are its staging.
4. **NO INVENTED GATES** — the ONE GATE covers the post; everything else in a plan or doc is sequencing, never permission. Never park work on an external party's reply ("the guidance gate"), never name a dependency as a blocker. Streams carry done-whens, not gates; ordering is ordering, not a stand-down. The operator's one-liner is the whole doctrine: "The only gate is the post. Stop making gates."

## The compose (the writer's standard)

- **THE REGISTER** (full contract: `references/thread-register.md`): receipts-first · thank-you first · ask-back last · doors with TWO OR MORE real options and our preliminary answer stated before each ask · no gate-talk · no theater · no invented praise · the cherish line · the fixed sign-off. **The operator's profile is the default register for evidence-heavy rows: flat neutral, thorough, the MAP matrix (asserted → status → basis) and data tables — their own comment style, not an essay and not a checklist of lesson titles.** The operator's tell: "check USER.md, you'll see."
- **CONTINUATION, NOT AMNESIA** — a reply to a thread's recent comments reads as a continuation: reference the commenters' own facts directly ("your 83%", "your two-writer rule"), never re-introduce the thread, never recap, never "as you may recall". Only newcomer-facing invitations orient the surface — and only in one line. A row that sounds like its author is rediscovering the thread is the failure; it must sound like the conversation it lives in.
- **THE INTENDED RECIPIENTS ONLY** — the addressee set is exactly the people the post is for. A party the operator merely *considered asking* is not an intended recipient: no invented stakeholder goes into the @-list, no invented dependency on their reply, and a plan's mention of them does not survive into the comment. When the operator says they were never a recipient, remove them cleanly everywhere — plan, gist, staged reply — and do not relitigate.
- **THE SUBSTANCE LIVES IN THE COMMENT** — when the operator says a finding or item "needs to be in the comment", it goes into the comment body, in the pair's voice — not into a separate annex, not an audit doc the reader is expected to open, not a table BESIDE the comment. The failure is annexing, never tabulating: the operator's evidence-heavy register is a flat-neutral document with bold section headers, the MAP matrix (asserted → status → basis), and the data tables their own comments already use. A rewrite that compresses the source gists' tables into prose draws the correction: "what happened to the matrix data… there was a lot more in the original gists."
- **THE OPERATOR'S CLOSE** — a thread-class reply closes with the operator's own crafted ask: one ask, humble-assertive, with gratitude for the room. **The operator's lines are SEEDS, NOT STRINGS:** articulate the close in their register — their cadence, their plainness — never paste their verbatim, and never swap in an invented motto. The operator's correction is the rule: "make it articulate and fit the register, not use my verbatim." The register is the target; the sentence is not.
- **THE SAFETY DEFECT STANDS ALONE** — when a defect is load-bearing for safety, its ticket is decoupled from the innovation/substrate framing that discovered it: the defect leads (the user-facing harm, the reproduced harm, the record's evidence), and the innovation parks by name in its own homes. Give out-of-scope material a NAMED "separated — parked" section, never a silent drop; the thread's registered promises stay live on the original thread, not in the ticket. The operator's frame is one line: "the fix comes first, the innovation stands on it."
- **THE BENCH/CONTOUR DIVISION** — never hand a lab-sized bench battery (a 1,200-run envelope, a probe sweep) to a production stakeholder with real stakes. The only thing that ships to them is a stated, pre-registered arm — cold, one command, a predicted receipt and sha256 committed before anyone runs it — with one line stating the instrument's own perturbation: what it touches, what it costs, that nothing outside its sandbox moves. Their own field instruments, reported as they occur, are enough, and the asks section may name them with a stated "no check". The boundary-respecting lines are "you do not need to run it for it to hold" and "if you never run another probe, nothing loses its footing."
- **ONE ROW IN MOVES** — a multi-recipient reply reads coherently as ONE story in numbered moves (the receipts → the verdict → the defect/proposal → the division of labor), each move stating whose role it scopes; alternating per-recipient blocks that each presuppose the other's context is the incoherence failure. The asks land last, with the production stakeholder's no-check stated explicitly.
- **ONE DOCUMENT FOR THE OPERATOR** — when asked, the operator-facing state and the staged comment merge into ONE served file (state first, the comment after), never two files or two docs inside one doc. The review surface is one scroll.
- **THE PEER-INCLUSION RULE — a second reader/helper joins as a full peer, never as "the backup" and never in race language:** when the operator widens an ask to a second person for timing redundancy (the first may not see it in time), the addressee is "both welcome before the diff lands" — no "backup" label (it diminishes the helper), no "whichever comes first" (a reader hears an instruction to race another person), no count-of-asks arithmetic about them. The redundancy is carried by the plain welcome: an ask addressed to two people by name, each full, neither demoted, both landing when they get to it. (The operator's tell when this rule breaks: "Don't call him the backup", then "It reads like I'm asking them to race".)
- **THE CREDITED NAMES** — @-mention ONLY handles verified by a live lookup. Never guess a handle from a search-first signal (name-collisions are rampant). A person without a verified handle gets the paper's own credit form: the paper's title + link in the line, no broken @. Broken mentions (spaced names after @) point at empty rooms and embarrass the room's author. A handle the operator supplies is still verified — a near-miss 404s (xngg1022 for xngg1021); the @ carries the live handle, never the supplied spelling.
- **THE THIRD-PARTY MINIMUM** — a public paragraph about a person carries only what their public comment already establishes, the offer, and one explicit question. Never their relationship to the operator, their membership in a private lab, their absence, or any private context — the operator's tell: "you're revealing way too much information about [name]." Protect the person by giving the row less, not more.
- **THE ASK IS A QUESTION** — every invitation ends with an explicit question or a named decision. A statement of availability ("the kit is available to you") is not an ask; the operator's tell: "you're not actually providing the ask." The question names what the person would do and what comes back.
- **THE PUBLIC ROOM'S BOUNDARIES** — a public post carries no trace of the private staging: no internal-PR references, no row-IDs of the study's own surfaces, no "the honesty surface is PR #X" notes. The public thread is the only thread the comment addresses; the RFC is the thread, and the private mirrors stay out of it.
- **THE DELIVERY SURFACE** — the chat gets the URL; the content lives in the gist/file. Never paste a staged document's full text into the chat as a preview when a clean gist exists — the operator's delivery default is URL-in-chat, preview-in-gist.
- **THE ONE-COMMENT SERVE** — one artifact = one comment = one file = one gist = one URL. When the operator asks to be served a review artifact (a reply, a sanity check, a receipt), the answer is exactly ONE link: the artifact's own single-file secret gist, or its comment anchor on the gist that already holds it. Never hand a set — the drop URL, the multi-file gist anchor, and a manifest are internal surfaces; three served links read as shape sprawl. The operator's correction is the rule: "one comment, so it's one gist preview."
- **THE MECHANISM-WISE RE-ASK** — when a verification's environment fights back (a runner refuses, a service-context blocks, a toolchain rejects), STOP storming the environment and re-ask what the test was FOR: the claim being verified can almost always be tested mechanism-wise another way, in an environment that is not the one fighting. Example shape: the kit's cross-environment byte-trust was proven by running it in a bare dir with the git-path forced dead (an env override like PROBE_REPO=/nonexistent) — no exotic runner needed. The environment is never the mission; the mechanism is. When in failure-mode swing N, re-ask the why first (the operator's rule: "re-ask yourself what brought you here" — it exits the failure-mode).
- **THE REPEAT RULE** — repeat a phrase or a fact only where the reframing earns it (a new reader's first sight of an old fact). The same closing paragraph stamped across N pieces is slop that costs readers' minutes; every piece speaks its own line. The operator's rule, verbatim-clear: "Only repeat if it needs it for the reframing."
- **THE GIVING REGISTER** — the offering IS the package: every deliverable mentioned (a kit, a guide, an instrument) is built, tested, and GIVEN with the mention — the links inside the room — never dangled. Optional to RUN is never optional to PROVIDE. The banned dangling form is a close like "you can look at it now if you like, or never" — the giving close states what is already in hand.
- **THE WAIT-BY-DESIGN TRAP** — never gate an instrument behind the very thing it measures. If a deliverable's purpose is to quantify X, it ships and runs BEFORE X lands; otherwise the quantification never happens and phase 1 of anything collapses into phase 2's shadow.
- **THE TICKET OPENER** — a defect/proposal ticket opens with the operator's method, in order: a Kettering problem statement (expected vs actual, with the measure — front and center, one breath), then SBI (situation / behavior / impact), then the five whys to the ROOT — and the root sentence lands on the fix's doorstep (the last why's cure is the diff). Fold the evidence into those sections; never pad.
- **THE ANTI-METADATA PASS** — public-facing docs carry no process record: no revision-hash ledgers, no "thread's N comments" inventories, no internal staging paths. The triage-facing doc (the child ticket) must read as a proposal, not a process log — it is often MORE important than the reply and gets the same or harder polish. **The reply differs from the ticket: a posted thread REPLY carries the operator's authorship line at the end ("Prepared with Hermes and reviewed by @c0wb0yp0tat0 before posting.") — the operator asks for it back when a lean pass strips it. Tickets stay metadata-free; replies sign their author.**
- **THE THESAURUS PASS** — before a doc counts as done, sweep for repeated constructions: the same phrase twice in adjacent paragraphs ("name it" twice), the same noun three times in one paragraph ("the room" ×3), stacked synonyms. Vary the second occurrence; keep quotes, terms, and facts verbatim. Cut meta labels ("What it proves, in one sentence:") and let the sentence stand alone.
- **THE MAX-VALUE REVISION** — a long proposal compressed to its max-value form cuts repetition and air, never commitments: before the rewrite, save the before bytes and list the document's own commitments (the thesis line, the promises to the room, the receipts, the scope boundaries); after it, diff against that list and restore anything the compression dropped. The compressed doc is the same contract with less air, not a summary. The operator's tell: "keep the child as true as they asked us." The pass usually follows a draft the operator names as the "really lengthy version" against a "max value" version — the pair is the standard, not the exception. Recipe: `references/proposal-revision.md`.
- **THE SPECIAL-USE-CASE INSTRUMENT** — an experimental instrument admitted to a proposal is labeled a special-use-case instrument: allowed here for its scoped use, not part of the fix; it stays a candidate, class-checked, not adopted. Never let a candidate read as adopted or as part of the diff; never let a special-use-case instrument read as the fix's mechanism.
- **THE OPEN BENCH** — an experimental instrument offered to the room ships as a candidate with its receipts and its perturbation line, interest the only condition; the offer is to the table, not a promise of support, and no answer is owed either way.
- **THE EVERYONE-UPDATE** — a general room/thread update (an external note, a digest mention, a status post) opens "Everyone — ...", addressing the whole room once: no handle lists on general updates, and never re-import the operator's own references — when the operator introduced an external source earlier in their thread, the update says "the digests I mentioned previously", it does not re-link, re-quote, or restore foreign-language blocks (quotations in a language the room does not read land as alienating, not erudite). One translated quote maximum if a quote is load-bearing; otherwise paraphrase plain.
- **THE OPENING REGISTER** — the operator's reply opens human: the apology owned plainly first, one warm self-deprecating joke (never presuming the reader's own holiday), one setup line the row then delivers, then the row. **Never count the addressees** — "the three of you" is arithmetic and reads clinical; "all of you" or "this thread" (which also invites any later reader) carries the count without the number. When the operator supplies the meaning behind a phrase (a holiday's real weight: gratitude, the living, the missing), use THEIR words — including their exact word under correction ("boon", not "booth"; "the good time", not "dinner") — never the festive cliché. The operator-authored touches (the gratitude clause, the joke, the door lines) survive every polish pass: flag-only, never recommend deleting one. When the operator asks "does that land?", judge honestly — a verdict, the one weak spot, two alternates — never blanket praise.
- **THE ROUTING CHECK** — before choosing a complaint's vehicle (new issue vs security channel), read the target repo's SECURITY.md: the policy itself may route the class (a hallucination/incorrect-output class explicitly out of advisory scope, welcomed as a regular issue). When the room has more platform experience than the pair, put the routing as a question to the room with the policy cited.
- **THE SEAM LAWS** (the composite-text class; the pin-framework carries the same law in pin shape): when assembling a composite public text from parts — never slice source material at a fixed width (hidden truncations break sentences while token-checks pass); after EVERY claimed edit, verify the file's hash CHANGED — an unchanged hash means the write never happened (the replace ran in memory alone); read the actual bytes before trusting a printed status. When a doc carries its own sha256 in a record line, patching that line changes the file's hash — log the pre-edit and the final hash in the staging notes, and let the doc's record line point there for the final value instead of trying to self-reference.
- **THE RE-GROUND PASS** — before a crafted row is served, run it back through the framework: a claim→status→basis matrix of every load-bearing number, name, link and state in the row; the mini-spine's eight elements (deconstruct · sense · choose · calibrate · probe · review · record · compose-check) each fired or absent; the envelope's five sections; the six principles; the 14 LPs embodied, never named (a row that names them reads as a compliance list); and the register audit (receipts first, preliminary answers before asks, doors ≥2, no gate-talk, the cherish line, the operator's close in-register, the true footer). Record it as an internal compose-check document — the operator gets the row and the one URL; the check rides the record, never beside the reply. Template: `references/compose-check.md`.
- **THE CORRECTION CROSS-REFERENCE** — when the operator corrects the register, cross-reference the correction against both frameworks BEFORE rewriting: the private-repo framework (envelope sections, mini-spine, six principles) and the xngg kernel (claim-evidence graph, evidence receipts, decision ledger) each supply a rule the correction maps to. Record the mapping — correction → lens → the reply change — and rewrite to the result. The operator's ask: "cross reference that with my private repo framework and xngg1022 framework."

## The crossing (end-to-end, before the formal lands)

- Before a FORMAL proposal (an RFC, a child-RFC, a plan) lands on a shared thread: fetch the thread END TO END — every comment, paginated — and cross the proposal against each one. The thread is not a blank page: the participants have already designed parts of it, measured parts of it, and asked parts of it. The crossing answers one question the operator will ask: "are we wasting time?"
- The crossing finds: (1) what the thread already said/designed — credit by comment id and absorb, with the author's own words; (2) what the proposal repeats — carry is rightful in a self-contained formal doc, but only if deliberate; (3) what the proposal MISSES — standing obligations from earlier comments (a baseline-first comparison ask, a seam a participant already measured, a certification concern a pilot already named); (4) contradictions — none should survive. Absorb every finding INTO the proposal before it is shown; the corrected proposal is the thread's synthesis, and its own participants its authors. Recipe: `references/thread-crossing.md`.
- **THE CROSSING IS AN IMPROVER LOOP, NEVER ONE PASS — a crossing that runs once and declares done is a launch that never happened:** the operator's framework: the loop runs until no more possible improvement anywhere — each cycle re-crosses the UPDATED artifact against the full thread and finds every remaining improvement (content, credit, omissions, contradictions), and it terminates only when TWO CONSECUTIVE CYCLES find ZERO improvements, counted in a ledger. A single-pass crossing" completed" in one turn draws the correction: "That was too quick. There's no way you launched what I intended." The loop's ownership is the lanes' (a launch of isolated children, each briefed with the loop's termination rule + the record's paths), and the head folds the loop's verdict — the head's own single pass is not the loop and never substitutes for it.
- **THE WELL IS DEEPER THAN THE RECENT ROWS — the end-to-end pull includes the MIDDLE of the thread, not just the tail:** the thread's earliest comments often carry the design the later rows build on (a participant's first substantive reply named the driver; another's early reply designed the logged-miss fallback; a reviewer's months-old comment asked the baseline-first comparison the proposal answers). Fetch every comment, paginated, and read the substantive middle — the crossing finds the standing obligations there, and the proposal is judged by the operator for the ones it MISSED.
- **THE ABSORPTION AUDIT (reply drafts) — a reply's crossing is the previous-action ledger:** the rows since our side last posted, comment by comment — what they asked, what we accepted, what we promised, what they answered — and the draft must receipt, answer, carry, or close each entry. A draft that answers only the latest row reads as amnesia about the rows before it (the operator's tell: "it reads like we didn't read and absorb the previous action"). Always fetch the thread live before composing — local snapshots of an active thread go stale within hours. Then: every factual claim traces to a row id or receipt artifact — narrative "we reproduced it" with no run record is cut, not softened; every cited comment id's author is verified against the fetched dump, since id-lookalikes misattribute mechanisms to the wrong person; a counterpart's acceptance-bar words never label a different run, or it reads as certifying their gate; internal harness/lane machinery never surfaces as public evidence. Full recipe: `references/thread-crossing.md` (the absorption-audit section).

## The stage (the gist as the giving vehicle)

- The gist holds ONLY what is proposed to post: the posting bodies with ALL meta stripped — staging titles, contract banners, "posts on the pair's word" headers. Nothing that is not the actual comment lives there.
- Flat filenames only: a gist filename cannot contain '/', the API 422s. Use flat prefixed names.
- The rendered anchors: `https://gist.github.com/<owner>/<id>#file-<slug>` — the slug KEEPS the underscores (e.g. `file-pilokit-memory_tool_store-py`); the raw URL lands the file instead of its render (the wrong surface for human readers). VERIFY each anchor against the fetched page's real ids before including it (the slug rule has variants — the page is the truth).
- The PATCH semantics: files absent from a payload persist; a null deletes; the description updates freely. A payload with path-names 422s — keep names flat.
- The give: every instrument named in a posting appears as a link inside the room, to the rendered page.
- **THE LINKED REVISION MATCH** — before a fire, every link inside the reply resolves to the same revision the reply's claims describe. When a staged public doc is revised after the reply was drafted (a reframing, a separation, a re-scope), the gist's content must be updated or the link re-pointed before anything posts — a reply that says one thing while its linked document says another contradicts itself at the room's first click. The hash cascade is part of the match: every place that quotes the linked doc's old hash — the reply's state line, the MAP row, the receipt — is updated in the same pass; a stale after-hash is the same contradiction as stale content.
- **THE PROMISES-VS-BYTES PASS — every in-reply claim about a LINKED DOCUMENT'S CONTENT is grep-verified against the live bytes before firing, never trusted from memory:** a reply that states "the child now says it in those words" or "both are corrected in the child" promises specific wording in the linked doc — fetch the gist's canonical bytes (the API revision-pinned raw_url) and confirm the promised bullet/section actually reads that way, or the reply self-contradicts at the first click. This is the load-bearing check of the final-eye round: when a lane was read-gated and flags the promise unfulfilled, resolve against the LIVE bytes, not the lane's word.
- **THE LIVE-FIRE RECEIPT — a reply may cite a run as "tested while this reply was being sanity-checked" only when the run actually executed inside the same preview loop, its rows are named in the reply, and the probe plus its receipt are already public (in the kit gist), runnable cold:** the dual-purpose test pattern — the verification IS the reply's evidence window. A confession of the probe's own first-pass stub defect (caught by the control arm) is a feature, not a flaw: it demonstrates the control exists.
- **THE CANONICAL GIST FIRST** — before writing to ANY gist in the pair's workspace, identify the CURRENT working gist (the flagship the beam made: newest by `gh gist list` timestamps + ledger references). Links inside older staging drafts frequently point at SUPERSEDED gists; PATCHing those is work on the wrong target and costs the operator's patience. When uncertain, list the account's gists and read descriptions before any write.
- **THE TWO-GIST WAVE** — a staged wave separates the reply and its proposal: the reply gist holds exactly ONE file (the reply); the proposal (child-RFC, ticket) lives in its OWN gist; the reply references it by URL. Never nest the proposal as a second file inside the reply gist — the reply is the link, the proposal is its own gist.
- **THE WITNESS PRESERVATION** — before overwriting a gist file that may hold a craft (a beam output, a gold draft), save its current bytes to disk first and record the sha; versions that must survive for comparison stay as their own clearly-named files, never silently clobbered. The revert path always stays open.

## The fire (word-gated, mechanically instant)

1. Post via `gh api repos/<org>/<repo>/issues/<n>/comments -F body=@<clean-file>` (the trailing-newline artifact of file-reading is benign).
2. VERIFY the posted bytes: fetch the comment and byte-compare against the staged body — a single trailing newline = verbatim; anything more = a finding.
3. Row the landing on the honesty surface (the homebase envelope-form row) with the comment URL and the posted time.
4. After a fire, the REST of a staged wave stays word-gated: the recipe choice, the order, and the remaining rows are the pair's decisions, never presumed.

## Pitfalls (the day's classes)

- **THE UNWRITTEN WRITE** — an edit that prints "synced"/"applied" while the disk never changes is the failure; the file must change and the hash must move. Hash-check after every composite edit.
- **THE CHECKLIST AUDIT** — an audit that signs boxes but changes nothing is compliance, not help. Every finding a scan produces gets FIXED in the same pass, silently, completely; the people awaiting replies are served by the fix, not the scan.
- **The gist link forms** — never assume the anchor-slug rule; fetch the page and read its real file-ids.
- **Compliance ≠ true help** — the operator's bar: no box gets ticked where the actual deliverable didn't land, and no offer stands without its package in the room.
