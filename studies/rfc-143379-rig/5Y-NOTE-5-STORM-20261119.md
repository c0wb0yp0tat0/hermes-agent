## The findings (the failure catalogue, receipted)

**FAILURE-CLASS-1 — THE COMPACTION CLIFF: EXACT CONTENT DECAYS ROUND OVER ROUND (arXiv 2608.22752, "The Compaction Cliff"):** on 20 production agent configurations, Claude Code's /compact on Sonnet 4.6 preserves **53% of safety rules after one round, 10% after five**; type-blind compaction has no signal for which lines are load-bearing and summarizes them at the same rate as surrounding text; the decay appeared for every LLM family and structural baseline tested. The guard exists: per-type triage (pin the load-bearing class, compact the rest) returns 96% recall over five rounds. Translation: the K-recency long-session reversal (−3.5pp at 300 cycles) and the post-compaction failure cluster (PersistGym's five-steps) are the field's measured class, not rig-specific bad luck; the countermeasure is the RFC's own archive-before-stub + provenance-origin classification — the field now has numbers for exactly that design (2–4× more retention, 96% recall). The probe must include the repeated-compaction axis — single-round performance is the happy path.

**FAILURE-CLASS-2 — THE MODEL-OR-HARNESS ATTRIBUTION PROBLEM (arXiv 2607.28802, interaction-centric taxonomy):** the same visible failure may call for model post-training, harness engineering, environment redesign, or benchmark repair — outcome-level labels cannot localize it; the taxonomy assigns each of 41 failure modes to a component edge with a fault side. The model–memory edge alone carries six modes: Missed Write, State Staleness, Overgeneralization, Memory Rationale Erosion, Pollution, Redundancy. Translation: if the probe shows the substrate failing, the failure may be the model's memory-use (write/read discipline), not the substrate — the oracle arm (NOTE-2's F6) is the attribution instrument: evidence-only turns separate "the model cannot use the fact" from "the substrate failed to deliver it." The probe must classify every failure by edge before reporting a substrate verdict.

**FAILURE-CLASS-3 — NO SINGLE SUBSTRATE DOMINATES — THE REGIME REVERSAL (arXiv 2608.15008, "Harness the Memory"):** 11 substrates × 3 backbones × 4 benchmarks: "no single substrate consistently dominates"; the optimal substrate reverses between regimes (structural graphs lead dialogue QA but are Pareto-dominated on agentic tasks); **excessive retrieval hurts sequential decision-making by shifting attention away from action-critical context**; substrates that perform well at moderate history lengths become costly or brittle at longer horizons. Translation: the doc-chain's claim is regime-scoped — the probe's non-re-observable-fact class is one regime; the honest negative possibility is that the substrate's win is regime-limited, and the probe must name its regime (long-conversation recall) rather than imply universal superiority. This is the might-not-work with the most teeth: the field measured dominant-substrate reversals with the same harness discipline the probe proposes.

**FAILURE-CLASS-4 — THE BENCHMARK ITSELF CAN MIS-SCORE (arXiv 2607.28367, "How Benchmarks Mis-Score"):** 15.3% of audited FAIL verdicts in 150 public trajectories are wrong — 10.7% evaluator false negatives, 4.7% broken tasks; WebArena's answer matching rejects acceptable behavior at 21.7% false negatives; a scalar success rate "cannot distinguish a wrong plan from a wrong click, a stale environment, or a checker error." Translation: the probe's judge/benchmark is a failure surface — the false-rejection trap (NOTE-1 F5) is measured in the wild (up to 21.7% per benchmark); the probe's episodes need human-audited verdicts on a sample, or the certification rides on a broken ruler again. The K-recency broken-ruler lesson is the rig's own instance of this field-wide class.

**FAILURE-CLASS-5 — MEMORY MAINTENANCE, NOT RETRIEVAL, IS WHERE SYSTEMS ROT (huggingface Memanto write-up; arXiv 2606.29914's cost axis):** "unmaintained stores converge on retrieving stale contradictions with high confidence"; knowledge updates and temporal reasoning are the discriminating categories — "the aggregate is mostly measuring the easy categories"; "a memory system that confidently answers from a stale or wrong memory is worse than one that says 'I don't have that'"; the temporal-bug checklist (silently-ignored filters, date off-by-ones, delete-recreate duplicates, timezone mixing). Translation: the probe should include the stale-contradiction class (a fact that changed, answered confidently) — the field says aggregate accuracy hides it; abstention is the honest behavior to measure. This maps the RFC's "you're not listening" to its failure class: confident stale answers are worse than abstention, and the committed-record design (provenance + recency) is the maintenance guard.

## The refinement to the 5Y (what this changes)
- The might-not-work list becomes a measured catalogue with a guard per class: the compaction cliff (round-decay: 53%→10%) says the probe must test repeated compactions, not one round; the attribution problem says every failure must classify by edge (model vs harness) before verdict; the regime reversal says the claim must be scoped by regime and the probe must name its regime; the mis-scoring benchmark says verdicts need human audit on a sample; the stale-contradiction class says the probe should include knowledge-update episodes and measure abstention.
- The honest negative is now a *named, receipted* possibility — the mission's optimism no longer depends on ignoring it, and each named failure carries its countermeasure (which is the probe's design, not a hope).
