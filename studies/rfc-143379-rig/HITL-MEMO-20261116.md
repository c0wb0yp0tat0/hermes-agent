## Part 3 — What was learned

### The learning of the surface

The study's surface is the place where the work lives and the place where the work is seen. What the eleven days showed is that a public surface, when it is held to a standard of completeness, becomes a tool for its own honesty. The thread did not need a supervisor to know when a row was missing; the one-line state, posted on a schedule, made the gaps visible to the machine itself. The disk guard — a mechanism that watches its own storage and enforces its own bound, and rows the enforcement — is the clearest example of that: the machine found its own limit and said so, without being asked.

The rings — five named improvements, each of them a small fix to a named part of the machinery — showed the same thing in miniature. The seam-fix ring, the reservation ring, the inference ring, the anchors ring, the seam ring. The anchors ring went from five-for-five to one-for-five, a real movement that the record kept rather than smoothed. The seam ring was marginal, at one point eight percent, and then it was cleared, the compaction loss-class healed at two point four percent. A loss class that heals is a loss class that can be trusted.

What this means for any reader trying to build their own verification surface is that the surface is not a log of events; it is a tool for self-correction. A surface that reports its own limits is a surface that can be believed.

### The named patterns

Beyond the five learnings, the arc banked six named patterns — each one a failure class that appeared, was caught, was named, and was refused from then on. They are the study's export to anyone building a similar rig, and each has its full history in the lessons bank.

**The phantom-completion pattern.** A result that looks complete but carries no proof at the byte level — the report that says done without the bytes to prove it. It appeared early and recurred; each time, the record's own re-read caught it, and the verdict was named: a landing is a claim until its bytes are checked. The discipline it became: every landing carries its receipt in the same breath as its claim. The arc's own first week is the proof that the pattern is real: more than one row landed with its claim ahead of its proof, and in each case the gap was found not by watching the machine work but by reading the record cold — the receipt was missing, and the missing receipt was the finding. The out-of-band evaluator exists because of these moments: a separate reader of real-world state, told to check what the lane narrated about itself, and to report the difference.

**The invented-gate pattern.** A gate that exists only in the telling, not in the record — an assertion that some check had been passed when no check's row existed. It was caught mid-arc by reading the record cold, and the discipline it became: a gate is a gate only when the record shows it. A claim of a gate without its row is a claim, not a gate. The correction rows of the sixth are its cousins: when the finish-line's true shape differed from the assumed one, the correction was posted before the work continued — the record accepted the correction and moved on, which is precisely how a gate gets its row. The distinction matters to anyone building on this study: a gate that cannot be shown is a risk, not a control.

**The stalled-driver pattern.** A lane sitting at its post with nothing queued, reading as healthy while idle — the stillness mistaken for health.
