## Part 3 — What was learned

Each lesson below is a named pattern, with when it was earned and where it is banked. The bank is the lessons file in the doctrine records; the thread and the day-by-day evidence file carry the receipts.

**The phantom-completion pattern.** A result that looks complete but carries no byte-level proof. Earned early, repeatedly. The discipline that beats it: a landing is a claim until its bytes are checked. Banked.

**The invented-gate pattern.** A gate that exists only in the telling, not in the record. Earned mid-arc; each instance was caught by re-reading the record cold. Banked.

**The stalled-driver pattern.** A lane sitting at its post with nothing queued, reading as healthy while idle. The fix: name the blocker. Banked.

**The recovery-from-a-failed-session pattern.** A session that died with its standing ask unprocessed could be recovered whole — the last user rows read verbatim from the store, the deck re-issued, the mission continued rather than restarted. This proved end to end. Banked.

**The split-verification pattern.** One verification surface could not carry a 133-row record alone; the lane split, each half landed its own section, and the sections joined into the complete record. Banked.

**The quality gates.** The runner-software hardenings — safe writes, no unsigned work, error isolation with deterministic abort — each named, each with its commit, each inherited by every later run.
