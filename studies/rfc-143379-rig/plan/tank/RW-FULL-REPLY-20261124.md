**And then the thing we changed, which we think your lab will find most useful: we stopped asserting provenance and started committing it.** The principle came from an unexpected place. One of us spent years mining CPU-mineable cryptocurrency, and what transferred was not the machinery but an abstraction. A commitment hash is a photograph of the evidence rather than a claim about it: slow to produce, cheap to verify, open to anyone, and independent of who authored it. It proves a thing existed at a time. We applied that to provenance itself, so the trail is hashed as it is made rather than reconstructed afterwards. Standard SHA-256 and concatenation, no new primitives.

That reframes what counts as evidence. An idea's value is not what its author says about it. It is the adoption trail, and the trail is observable.
