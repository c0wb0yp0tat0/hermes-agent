*Storm doc for 5Y-NOTE-7. The note asked: why are the free tiers the key? The storm answers with the field's harness-leverage economics — and returns a refinement that is also a correction: the leverage is real and quantified, but it is regime-scoped and carries a coordination tax the mission's calendar must price.*

## The findings (mechanism per class, receipted)

**F1 — THE LEVERAGE IS MEASURED, TWICE (arXiv 2607.06906 Harness Effect, r=0.99, already banked; arXiv 2607.08938 "90% Cheaper Agents"):** the Harness Effect's r=0.99 (weaker model → harness lifts quality more) is now joined by a direct small-model study: meta-discovered harness adaptation improves 16 of 21 SLM-task pairs, seven close the SLM–LLM gap, and the best SLM agent **recovers 89.7% of LLM performance at 4% of the cost.** Caveats named by the same paper: adaptation works best on less-diverse tasks (Spearman ρ=−0.96 — the more repetitive the workflow, the more the harness absorbs) and for SLMs with stronger base capabilities (+48.8% vs +15.5%). And the completion-truth warning (arXiv 2611.21407, the 0.75B–4.7B ladder): "a scorer that reads exit status credits work that was never written" — 85 of 127 artifact-less runs exit normally. Translation: the fleet's free seats are the regime where harness leverage is strongest, and the rig's lane-products (artifacts on disk, write-time asserts, byte-verified scribe cells) are exactly the completion-truth the field warns is usually missing. The key is not just cheapness — it is that the harness work the rig already does converts cheap seats into near-frontier output.

**F2 — THE HARNESS IS A LARGER COST LEVER THAN THE MODEL (Harness Effect, F1):** "moving from the most to the least expensive model under the baseline saves 36%, whereas retaining any model and adopting the harness saves 33% to 61%" — and the largest relative gains occur in the fast tier (Flash 3.5 falls 61% cost, 55% latency) because harness overhead is a bigger share of a cheap model's bill. Translation: NOTE-7's root ("the weaker the seat, the more the harness lifts it") extends to the dollar side — the harness is the biggest lever on the expensive-seat bill too, and the fleet's harness discipline applies to every seat at once. The free tiers aren't just the key; the harness that makes them strong is the key to the whole bill.

**F3 — THE COORDINATION TAX IS REAL — PREASSIGNED TEAMS, NOT DECENTRALIZED (arXiv 2603.12229, "Language Model Teams as Distributed Systems"):** distributing work across agents follows Amdahl's law (parallel tasks win; serial tasks gain nothing and pay 5.83× tokens for 1.13× speedup); **preassigned teams beat decentralized teams** (median speedup 1.36× vs 0.88×) — "decentralized coordination often reduces efficiency"; token costs scale with team size (ρ=0.40) while speedup does not (ρ=−0.07). And HiveMind (arXiv 2604.17111): uncoordinated parallel agents on a shared rate-limited endpoint fail at 72–100% under contention; OS-style scheduling (admission control, rate-limit tracking, AIMD backpressure, retry) reduces failures to 0–18% and eliminates 48–100% of wasted compute — **transparent retry is the single most critical primitive.** Translation: the fleet's preassigned-lens + envelope structure is the field-verified coordination shape (preassigned > decentralized), and the harness's 429-backoff/write-window read-gate IS HiveMind's scheduling primitives, already built. The window-calendar line needs the coordination cost priced: parallel fan-outs on windowed seats must be preassigned by the envelope, and the retry discipline is the contended-endpoint lifeline.

**F4 — THE HETEROGENEOUS-TEAM CORRECTION (arXiv 2504.02051, Self-Resource Allocation; GEAR SSRN):** mixing many weaker models can reduce average efficiency — but "including at least one stronger model in a heterogeneous team improves efficiency" (Qwen+GPT-4o-mini 4.04 vs 2×GPT-4o-mini 2.79); explicit capability info improves planners' allocation with suboptimal workers; GEAR: outer escalation with adaptive routing reduces token consumption without compromising reasoning. Translation: the fleet's flagship-compose design (one strong head reading all lanes' landings) is the field-verified heterogeneous team shape — the free tiers as workers + the flagship as the escalating stronger member. The key of "the free tiers are the key" is the PARTNERSHIP with a stronger seat, not the tiers alone.

**F5 — THE FREE-LANE DISCIPLINE IS MEASURED AND BORING-REAL (dev.to "$0 inference stack", checked 2026-11-14):** the free lanes (Cloudflare 10,000 neurons/day resets 00:00 UTC; Nous Portal free models like stepfun/step-3.7-flash:free; OpenRouter :free tags; AMD 10-point/30-day; Colab T4 session-scoped) work when disciplined: "match the model to the job, and never let an unattended loop loose on a metered lane." The catches match the rig's window-calendar line exactly: resets at 00:00 UTC, quotas shrink/rename, fallbacks required. "Free tiers shrink, rename models, and add queues. Anything you build on them needs a fallback and a monthly re-check." Translation: the window-calendar is the field's own operating requirement — the fleet's seats ARE the free-lane economy, and the calendar line is not a convenience but the survival discipline of the whole shape.

**F6 — THE REGIME CAVEAT (the honest negative for NOTE-7):** the leverage axis is regime-scoped (F1's ρ=−0.96: repetitive workflows, not diverse novel ones; the manazir.dev synthesis: the harness buys "roughly one model generation, a 2–6× multiplier" — then hits a model-bound ceiling: "once a competent harness exists, swapping the model dominates every other intervention"; ORDER: build retrieval/harness first, then the model swap dominates). Translation: the free tiers are the key FOR THE WORK THE PROBE SHAPES (structured, verifiable, repetitive-armed) — and the field's ordering rule (harness first, then escalate the model) is the mission's sequence: harness-then-probe-then-flagship. The eventual model-swap dominance is the Open Door, not a refutation.
