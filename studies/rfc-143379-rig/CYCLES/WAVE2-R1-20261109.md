## 1. The Storm's R1 Harvest (design-input, summarized)

The webstorm's R1 item, as carried into WAVE2, is the write-gate's **MemHarmBench-citation**:

- **Claim line:** *Authority ≠ Retention ≠ Adoption.*
- **Headline figure carried in the storm:** **55.71% stealthy** — i.e., in the cited bench harvest, a majority of the sampled harmful/memori­zed items persisted in stealthy form, retained downstream of the point at which their authority had lapsed or been revoked in the source chain.
- **Why R1 matters to the write-gate:** R1 is not a claim about whether memorized content exists. It is a claim about *provenance discipline*: a thing can be **retained** (still present in the store/context) long after it has lost **authority** (no current owner has authorized its continued presence for this purpose), and can later be **adopted** (pulled into a new decision/output) by a downstream step that never checked either predecessor. The 55.71% stealthy rate is the empirical caution that silent retention is the norm, not the exception — a write-gate that only checks at write-time and never re-derives provenance will ship stealthy carries.
- **Design consequence drawn in the storm:** the write-gate's **Stage-2 provenance** must not be a single boolean ("was this ever authorized?"). It must be a three-part provenance row that can state, independently: (a) authority, (b) retention, (c) adoption — each with its own timestamp, owner, and evidence pointer, so a stealthy carry is legible as a carry instead of laundering itself as fresh authority.

No new empirical claim is introduced by this draft. The storm's number and the authority/retention/adoption split are cited as *already-harvested design-input*, not re-litigated here.

## 2. The Write-Gate's Stage-2 Design-Lines (the lines this draft would defend)

Stage-2 (provenance) is drafted against three lines, each traceable to R1:

1. **Authority line:** Every retained item carries an authority pointer — who authorized its presence, under what scope, and until when. Authority is *current*, not historical; a revoked or expired authority renders the item retained-but-unauthorized, not adopted.
2. **Retention line:** Every retained item carries a retention pointer — where it lives, its TTL / review date, and the steward responsible for its continued storage. Retention without a live authority pointer is flagged, not silently renewed.
3. **Adoption line:** Every downstream use carries an adoption pointer — the decision/output that pulled the item in, and the provenance row it was pulled from. Adoption re-checks authority *at adoption time*; it does not inherit authority from retention.

Design test (from R1): if the gate cannot distinguish, for a given item, "retained but no longer authorized" from "authorized for this adoption," Stage-2 is not yet provenance — it is a log of the first write.
