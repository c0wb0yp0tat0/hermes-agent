## 3. The Masonic question, re-framed with the nuance it deserves

**The binary question ("was the seal Masonic?") is itself the skewed frame's question.** What the documents actually support:

- **Documented non-membership:** of everyone on the three design committees, only Franklin is a documented Mason — and his committee's design was rejected. Thomson, Barton, and du Simitière have no documented membership. (Barton is sometimes confused with a different William Barton who was a Mason; Hopkinson's alleged connection has no firm evidence.)
- **The symbol predates the lodge usage:** the Eye of Providence was a common Christian/Enlightenment emblem long before the seal; it enters standard Masonic iconography only in 1797 (Webb's *Freemason's Monitor*), fifteen years after 1782.
- **The authoritative Masonic-side statement in the files:** S. Brent Morris (Masonic Service Association, 1995): "The Great Seal of the United States is not a Masonic emblem, nor does it contain hidden Masonic symbols… The combining of the eye of providence overlooking an unfinished pyramid is a uniquely American, not Masonic, icon."
- **The historians' actual conclusion is not "not Masonic" but "parallel sources"** (Patterson & Dougall): the founders and the Masons drew on the same 18th-century symbolic language — Providence imagery, classical poetry (Virgil), emblem books, the Enlightenment's shared visual vocabulary. Both were reading from the same book of symbols; that is a cultural overlap, not a conspiracy.

**What remains honestly open:**

- Why Thomson chose the *seclorum* spelling — no manuscript explains it.
