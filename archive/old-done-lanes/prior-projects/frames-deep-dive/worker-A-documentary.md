# Worker A — Documentary/Historical Frame: *Novus Ordo Seclorum* & *Opus Dei*

**Scope of this frame.** This reconstruction covers only what the verifiable documentary and scholarly record establishes about two Latin phrases — *novus ordo seclorum* (reverse of the Great Seal of the United States) and *opus dei* (a Catholic institution and a Latin theological phrase). It does not evaluate conspiracy claims or pop-culture readings (covered by other workers); it reports only primary sources, official documents, mainstream journalism and scholarship, and it says explicitly when something cannot be verified.

**MAP legend.** Every assertion carries a tag: `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` (documented in the cited source), `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` (the documentary record contradicts it), or `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` (no primary source located; or inherently a matter of faith/interpretation). A basis URL follows each tag.

## PART 1 — *Novus ordo seclorum* (US Great Seal, reverse)

### 1.1 What the documentary record establishes (timeline of primary sources)

**Design committees (1776–1782).** On July 4, 1776, hours after adopting the Declaration of Independence, the Continental Congress resolved "that Dr. Franklin, Mr. J. Adams, and Mr. Jefferson, be a committee, to bring in a device for a seal for the United States of America." That first committee's design was tabled; a second committee (James Lovell, John Morin Scott, William Churchill Houston) sat in 1780 and was also tabled; a third committee sat in May 1782 (John Rutledge, Arthur Middleton, Elias Boudinot), relying on William Barton. On June 13, 1782, Secretary of the Continental Congress Charles Thomson was given all three committees' work; he produced a fourth design, revised by Barton, and submitted a written description ("blazon" plus "Remarks and Explanation") that the Continental Congress adopted on June 20, 1782. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — National Archives milestone-document page (item is Charles Thomson's 1782 design, Record Group 360, catalog id 595257): https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/original-design-of-the-great-seal-of-the-united-states ; US State Dept, *The Great Seal of the United States* (Bureau of Public Affairs brochure, 2003), pp. 2–5: https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/27807.pdf (retrieved via Internet Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/2018id_/https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/27807.pdf) ; US National Museum of American Diplomacy: https://diplomacy.state.gov/the-great-seal/

**The mottos and their Virgil sources.** Gaillard Hunt's official history (1909; Dept of State; the standard account until superseded by Patterson & Dougall, *The Eagle and the Shield*, 1978) reproduces Thomson's design and reports that on the reverse Thomson "adopted Barton's device, changing only the mottoes. These were from Virgil, 'Audacibus annue coeptis' (favor my daring undertaking), being found in the Aeneid, book 9, verse 625 (also in the Georgics, i, 40), and 'Magnus ab integro seclorum nascitur ordo' (the great series of ages begins anew), coming from the fourth eclogue, fifth verse." `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Gaillard Hunt, *The History of the Seal of the United States* (Washington: Dept of State, 1909), p. 34, full text: https://archive.org/stream/historyofsealofu00unit/historyofsealofu00unit_djvu.txt (catalog record: https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/001963020)

The classical text of Virgil's Eclogue 4, line 5 reads: "magnus ab integro saeclorum nascitur ordo" ("the great cycle of ages is born anew"). `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — P. Vergili Maronis, Ecloga Quarta, The Latin Library: https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/vergil/ec4.shtml ; Perseus/Scaife reader, Eclogues 4: https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:latinLit:phi0690.phi001.perseus-lat2:4/ ; translation as given at Wikipedia, *Novus ordo seclorum*: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novus_ordo_seclorum

**The official blazon and Thomson's own explanation.** The 1782 resolution (as reproduced in Hunt from *Journals of Congress*, vol. 4, p. 39) blazons the reverse: "A pyramid unfinished. In the zenith, an eye in a triangle, surrounded with a glory proper. Over the eye these words, 'Annuit Coeptis.' On the base of the pyramid the numerical letters MDCCLXXVI. And underneath the following motto, 'Novus Ordo Seclorum.'" Thomson's accompanying "Remarks and Explanation" (in his handwriting) states: "The pyramid signifies Strength and Duration: The Eye over it & the Motto allude to the many signal interpositions of providence in favour of the American cause. The date underneath is that of the Declaration of Independence and the words under it signify the beginning of the new American Æra, which commences from that date." `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Hunt 1909, pp. 40–43 (above URL); the same explanation is quoted verbatim in the State Dept 2003 brochure, p. 6 and p. 15 (reverse description: "Novus Ordo Seclorum (A new order of the ages)… signify the beginning of the new American era in 1776"): https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/27807.pdf

**Grammar and the "seclorum" spelling.** Grammatically, "novus ordo" is nominative singular ("a new order") and "seclorum" is the genitive plural of *saeculum* ("of the ages"); the standard rendering is "a new order of the ages." On spelling: Hunt 1909 (p. 34, footnote by Henry Livingston Thomas, late translator, Dept of State) notes, "Although the form 'seclorum' was adopted, the more approved spelling is 'saeclorum.'" The scholarly reference treatment (Wikipedia, citing Lewis & Short) states the syncopated forms *saecla*, *saeclorum* "were normal alternatives to the more common saecula etc. throughout the history of Latin poetry and prose," and that "the form saeculorum is impossible in hexameter verse"; it further notes that *seclorum* does not mean "secular" — *secularis* ("worldly") is a different word, while *seclorum* is the possessive plural of *saeculum*. So the spelling is a legitimate classical/poetic syncopation, not an error and not the word "secular." `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Hunt 1909 p. 34 (above URL); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novus_ordo_seclorum (with its citations to the State Dept brochure and Lewis & Short); classical text at The Latin Library and Perseus (above URLs).

**Official renderings used today.** The official description of the reverse in the State Dept's current brochure: "The pyramid signifies strength and duration: The eye over it and the motto, Annuit Coeptis (He [God] has favored our undertakings), allude to the many interventions of Providence in favor of the American cause. The date underneath is that of the Declaration of Independence, and the words under it, Novus Ordo Seclorum (A new order of the ages), signify the beginning of the new American era in 1776." `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — State Dept 2003 brochure, p. 15: https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/27807.pdf

**The reverse was never cut as a die.** Although the reverse was adopted by law in 1782, no die of it has ever been cut; the first drawings appeared in 1786 (James Trenchard, *Columbian Magazine*) and 1856 (Benson J. Lossing, *Harper's*), and the current official design follows Lossing's drawing as seen on the one-dollar bill. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — State Dept 2003 brochure, p. 12 and p. 10, n. 3: https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/27807.pdf ; National Archives milestone page ("the less familiar reverse, which is never used as a seal"): https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/original-design-of-the-great-seal-of-the-united-states

**The 1935 one-dollar bill adoption.** Two documented accounts exist, and they are complementary rather than contradictory:
- (a) The State Dept's official account: "In 1935, the Department of the Treasury sent President Roosevelt a new design for the bill, incorporating the obverse and reverse of the Great Seal. After approving it rather routinely, the President changed his mind, scratched out his signature, and inked in several significant changes. He switched the obverse and reverse and added 'The Great Seal'… and 'of the United States'… and initialed the whole 'FDR.'" The Bureau of Engraving and Printing noted receipt "June 26, 1935." The first bill to carry both sides was the Series 1935 $1 silver certificate. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — State Dept 2003 brochure, pp. 13–14: https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/27807.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depictions_of_the_Great_Seal_of_the_United_States
- (b) Henry A. Wallace's personal account (he was Secretary of Agriculture in FDR's cabinet): in 1934 he saw a pamphlet on the Great Seal by Gaillard Hunt that included a full-color copy of the reverse, which he had never seen; he liked the motto *Novus Ordo Seclorum* ("New Order of the Ages"), likened it to the New Deal, and suggested to Roosevelt that the reverse be used (Roosevelt chose the dollar bill rather than a coin). `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` (as Wallace's own account) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depictions_of_the_Great_Seal_of_the_United_States (section "The 1909 reverse", citing Wallace's account). Note: the State Dept's own account does not mention Wallace; the Wallace version rests on his later recollection.

**What the designers intended — historians' reading.** The documentary record is unambiguous that Thomson intended the phrase to mark the beginning of the American republic: his "Remarks and Explanation" says the words "signify the beginning of the new American Æra, which commences from that date [1776]." The State Dept's official interpretation is identical. No primary document from the 1782 designers or from the 1935 adoption assigns any "world government" meaning to the phrase. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Hunt 1909, pp. 42–43; State Dept 2003 brochure pp. 6, 15 (URLs above).

### 1.2 What remains unverifiable

- **Why Thomson chose the "seclorum" spelling.** Hunt's 1909 footnote (by the State Dept translator Henry Livingston Thomas) records only that "the form 'seclorum' was adopted" while "the more approved spelling is 'saeclorum'"; no manuscript from Thomson explaining his choice has been located in the cited record. `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` — Hunt 1909 p. 34 (above URL); no contrary primary source found.
- **The precise decision path for the 1935 dollar bill.** The physical evidence is FDR's annotated model (initialed "FDR", received June 26, 1935) and Wallace's later recollection; no contemporary presidential memorandum explaining the reverse's inclusion is cited in the official history. `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` — State Dept 2003 brochure pp. 13–14; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depictions_of_the_Great_Seal_of_the_United_States
- **The engraver of the 1782 die.** The State Dept history states the engraver "has not been positively identified (possibly Robert Scot of Philadelphia)." `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` — State Dept 2003 brochure p. 8 (above URL).

### 1.3 Where this frame contradicts the other frames

- **"New World Order" / world-government reading.** The conspiratorial frame reads *novus ordo seclorum* as a plan for a one-world government. The documentary record contradicts this: Thomson's 1782 explanation says the motto "signify[ies] the beginning of the new American Æra," and the official State Dept interpretation says the same; the phrase was designed in 1782, 153 years before it appeared on currency, and its 1935 appearance is documented as a Treasury/FDR design decision, not a hidden-hand insertion. `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` — Hunt 1909 pp. 42–43; State Dept 2003 brochure pp. 6, 13–15 (URLs above).
- **Masonic-origin claims.** The conspiratorial frame sometimes asserts the seal (Eye of Providence, mottos) was created by Freemasons. The documentary record: the Eye of Providence was a common Christian/Enlightenment motif in 18th-century Europe and America; per Masonic researcher David Barrett, the Eye was only used sporadically by Masons in the 1770s–80s and did not become a common Masonic symbol until 1797; of the men whose designs were adopted (Thomson, Barton, du Simitière, Hopkinson), none is documented as a Mason, and the only known Mason on any committee (Franklin) had his ideas rejected. `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` — Wikipedia, *Great Seal of the United States*, "Conspiracy theories" section with citations: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Seal_of_the_United_States
- **Pop-culture frame.** Pop-culture treatments (e.g., the "New World Order" meme) treat the motto as occult/globalist. The documentary frame shows a classical Latin motto, drawn from Virgil's Fourth Eclogue, officially glossed by its designer as the start of the American era. `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` — sources as above.

## PART 2 — *Opus Dei* (Catholic institution and theological phrase)

### 2.1 What the documentary record establishes

**(a) The phrase in Catholic theology.** In the Church's own usage, *opus Dei* ("the work of God") is the traditional name for the Divine Office / Liturgy of the Hours. The Rule of St Benedict (ch. 43, "Of those who come late to the Work of God or to table") contains the injunction "Ergo nihil Operi Dei praeponatur" ("Therefore let nothing be preferred to the Work of God"), in the context of the hour of the Divine Office (*hora Divini Officii*). The USCCB states: "The Liturgy of the Hours, also known as the Divine Office or the Work of God (Opus Dei), is the daily prayer of the Church." `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Rule of St Benedict, Latin text (1659 edition), Caput XLIII: https://dn790000.ca.archive.org/0/items/TheRuleOfStBenedict/TheRuleOfStBenedict.pdf ; https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/liturgy-of-the-hours ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours

**(b) The institution's founding and name.** Opus Dei was founded on 2 October 1928 in Madrid by the Spanish priest Josemaría Escrivá (1902–1975); per the prelature's own account, during a retreat he experienced what he described as an intellectual vision of the Work. The name *Opus Dei* (Latin, "Work of God") was chosen to signal that the undertaking was God's work rather than Escrivá's. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Opus Dei, "The Founding of Opus Dei": https://opusdei.org/en/article/the-founding-of-opus-dei/ ; Opus Dei FAQ ("The name is Latin for 'Work of God'."): https://opusdei.org/en-us/faq/opus-dei/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei (founding date, name rationale, with citations).

**(c) Status in canon law — timeline.**
- 1947: decree of praise from Pope Pius XII, making Opus Dei an institute of pontifical right; 1950: definitive approval (married laypeople admitted; secular clergy associated through the Priestly Society of the Holy Cross). `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei (citing Berglar, *Opus Dei*, 1994); Opus Dei FAQ (above).
- 28 November 1982: Pope John Paul II's apostolic constitution *Ut sit* erected Opus Dei as a personal prelature — the first (and so far only) personal prelature of the Catholic Church. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Pope Francis, motu proprio *Ad charisma tuendum* (14 July 2022), which restates this and confirms "the only one erected so far": https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20220714-motu-proprio-ad-charisma-tuendum.html ; Opus Dei, "Prelature of Opus Dei": https://opusdei.org/en/article/prelature-of-opus-dei/
- 14 July 2022 (in force 4 August 2022): Pope Francis's motu proprio *Ad charisma tuendum* modified *Ut sit*: competence for personal prelatures moved from the Congregation for Bishops to the Dicastery for the Clergy; the prelate must submit an annual report; and "the Prelate shall not be honoured with the episcopal order" (he receives instead the title of Supernumerary Apostolic Protonotary / Monsignor). `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — full text at the Vatican: https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20220714-motu-proprio-ad-charisma-tuendum.html ; Vatican News report: https://www.vaticannews.va/en/vatican-city/news/2022-07/pope-francis-motu-proprio-opus-dei.html

**(d) Members and statistics.** The figures are the prelature's own, and they vary by source and date: the Opus Dei FAQ states "about 96,000 members of Opus Dei worldwide" (priests roughly 2% of membership) — retrieved for this report; the BBC (2005) reported Opus Dei "claims around 85,000 members around the world, one third of them in Spain"; Wikipedia's infobox records 85,000 (2025), citing the same FAQ page, and members in more than 90 countries. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` (as self-reported figures; see 2.2 for the caveat) — https://opusdei.org/en-us/faq/opus-dei/ ; http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei

**(e) Escrivá's beatification and canonization.** Escrivá died 26 June 1975. His cause was opened in the early 1980s (nihil obstat granted 1981; process in Rome and Madrid 1981–84); he was beatified by John Paul II on 17 May 1992 and canonized on 6 October 2002 in St. Peter's Square. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — Vatican homilies: canonization (6 Oct 2002): http://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/homilies/2002/documents/hf_jp-ii_hom_20021006_escriva.html ; beatification (17 May 1992), homily: https://opusdei.org/en/article/homily-of-john-paul-ii-at-the-beatification-of-josemaria-escriva/ ; procedural timeline: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization_of_Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1

**(f) Documented controversies and how they are documented.**
- **Speed and process of the canonization.** Critics (e.g., Kenneth L. Woodward in *Newsweek*, January 1992; a leaked 1989 consultor's vote published by *Il Regno* in 1992) argued the cause moved too fast, that hostile witnesses were not heard, and that procedures were short-circuited; the postulator and supporters responded that the process was legal, unusually large (92 witnesses, a 6,000-page *Positio*), and consistent with post-1983 streamlined rules (St Thérèse's process also took 27 years). John L. Allen Jr. concludes Opus Dei "may have played hard and fast, but they played by the rules." The institution's own FAQ answers the question "How did Josemaria Escriva get canonized so quickly?" by noting he was canonized 27 years after his death and that the Church simplified procedures after Vatican II (JPII himself was canonized 9 years after death). `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` (both the criticisms and the responses are documented) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization_of_Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1 (with its citations to Woodward/Newsweek and Il Regno); https://opusdei.org/en-us/faq/opus-dei/ ; John L. Allen Jr., *Opus Dei* (2005), p. 265, via https://archive.org/details/opusdeiobjective00alle
- **"Catholic Mafia" / "Holy Mafia" allegations.** The label circulates in media (e.g., Der Spiegel, "Der Aufstieg der Santa Mafia," 2005; The Scotsman, "Secretive sect dubbed 'Mafia shrouded in white'," 2005). Mainstream reporting documents the allegation and the denials: in the BBC's 2005 feature, former member/sociologist Alberto Moncada says members "don't act together like a mafia. That's not true," and Opus Dei spokesman Jack Valero calls claims of influence in the Vatican "exaggerated." `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` (that the allegation exists and is contested) — http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm ; https://www.spiegel.de/spiegelspecial/a-350114.html ; https://www.scotsman.com/news/uk-news/secretive-sect-dubbed-mafia-shrouded-in-white-2510155
- **Ex-member accounts.** María del Carmen Tapia, a former member who worked in Opus Dei's Rome offices, published *Beyond the Threshold: A Life in Opus Dei* (Continuum, 1997); her account (excerpted by The New York Times) describes her 1950s experiences in Madrid, the CSIC/Arbor milieu, and her later break with the organization. Other former members (e.g., Carmen Charo, quoted by the BBC) allege strict control in numerary centers; the prelature denies these characterizations. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` (as the memoirist's first-person account and as a documented allegation/dispute) — https://www.nytimes.com/books/first/t/tapia-threshold.html ; http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm
- **The Da Vinci Code aftermath (note on dating).** The popular frame peaked with Dan Brown's *The Da Vinci Code*, published by Doubleday in 2003 (not 1997–2001), with the film in 2006. The documentary record of the aftermath: the BBC (2005) noted the novel "raised public interest in the controversial Catholic lay group Opus Dei, depicting it as a sect focused on wealth and power"; Opus Dei issued public responses; John L. Allen Jr.'s journalistic study *Opus Dei: An Objective Look Behind the Myths and Reality of the Most Controversial Force in the Catholic Church* (2005) was written partly in that context; The New York Times (2006) reported Opus Dei's public-relations response to the film. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Da_Vinci_Code (publication 2003); https://archive.org/details/opusdeiobjective00alle ; https://www.nytimes.com/2006/02/07/national/07opus.html (cited via the canonization article's reference list)
- **Recent investigative reporting.** In 2024, El País published an investigative series on Opus Dei ("Manipulation, greed and power: the untold story of Opus Dei," 6 Oct 2024), and Gareth Gore published the book *Opus* (Simon & Schuster, 2024); the prelature published formal responses. This is the current state of the documentary controversy stream. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — https://english.elpais.com/international/2024-10-06/manipulation-greed-and-power-the-untold-story-of-opus-dei.html ; Opus Dei response: https://opusdei.org/en-ph/article/response-book-opus-gareth-gore-simon-schuster/

**(g) What mainstream scholarship and Vatican documents actually say.** Vatican documents describe Opus Dei as a canonical structure of the Church (a personal prelature) with a specific charism: spreading "the universal call to holiness in the world, through the sanctification of work and family and social commitments" (*Ad charisma tuendum*). Independent scholarship treats it as a real but contested institution: sympathetic-to-critical academic and journalistic accounts (Allen 2005; Joan Estruch, *Saints and Schemers: Opus Dei and its Paradoxes*, Oxford UP, 1995; Michael Walsh, *Opus Dei: An Investigation*, 2004) document both its internal coherence and its controversies, without treating it as a secret world-governing body. `ASSERTED->VERIFIED` — https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20220714-motu-proprio-ad-charisma-tuendum.html ; https://archive.org/details/opusdeiobjective00alle ; https://archive.org/details/saintsschemersop0000estr

### 2.2 What remains unverifiable

- **Membership numbers.** All published figures (≈85,000–96,000; one-third in Spain; >90 countries) originate with the prelature or with press reports citing it; there is no independent audit, and the number on the prelature's own FAQ has shifted over time (85,000 in 2025; "about 96,000" in 2026). `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` — https://opusdei.org/en-us/faq/opus-dei/ ; http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei
- **Internal practices (mortification, control, finances).** Practices such as the cilice and discipline are acknowledged by the prelature itself; allegations of coercion, brainwashing, and financial pressure rest on ex-member testimony and investigative journalism, and are denied by the institution; no neutral adjudication exists in the cited record. `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` (documented as competing claims) — https://opusdei.org/en-us/faq/opus-dei/ ; http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm ; https://www.nytimes.com/books/first/t/tapia-threshold.html
- **Supernatural claims.** Escrivá's 1928 "vision" and the miracles certified for beatification/canonization are matters of religious testimony and canonical procedure, not of documentary verification; the documentary record establishes that these claims were made and processed, not that they are true. `ASSERTED->UNVERIFIABLE` (by design of this frame) — https://opusdei.org/en/article/the-founding-of-opus-dei/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization_of_Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1

### 2.3 Where this frame contradicts the other frames

- **"Secret church within a church" / "Holy Mafia" controlling world affairs.** The conspiracy frame treats Opus Dei as a hidden power bloc. The documentary record shows a public, canonically defined structure (personal prelature) with a modest self-reported membership (tens of thousands), under explicit Vatican governance, and subject to 2022 restructuring by Pope Francis (transfer to the Dicastery for the Clergy, removal of episcopal rank for the prelate, mandatory annual reports) — institutional supervision that is hard to square with an all-powerful hidden order. Mainstream reporting documents the "mafia" charge as an allegation that even the charge's cited sources dispute. `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` — https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20220714-motu-proprio-ad-charisma-tuendum.html ; http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm
- **Pop-culture frame (Da Vinci Code).** The novel/film depict Opus Dei as a murderous, conspiratorial sect with an albino assassin-monk. The documentary record contradicts this: Opus Dei is a Catholic prelature of lay people and priests whose members work in ordinary professions; the BBC, Allen, and the NYT coverage document the gap between the fictional depiction and the institution's public, documented self-presentation and legal status. `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` — http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/europe/4249444.stm ; https://archive.org/details/opusdeiobjective00alle ; https://www.nytimes.com/books/first/t/tapia-threshold.html (Tapia's own critical memoir also describes an ordinary, if strict, religious milieu, not the novel's fantasy)
- **Conflation of the two phrases.** Some frames conflate *novus ordo seclorum* (a US motto from Virgil, 1782, meaning "new order of the ages") with *Opus Dei* (a Catholic prelature, 1928, "Work of God") to imply a shared hidden agenda. The documentary record shows two unrelated phrases with unrelated provenances (pagan classical poetry for one; monastic/Christian usage for the other) and unrelated histories. `ASSERTED->CONTRADICTED` — sources in Parts 1 and 2 above.
