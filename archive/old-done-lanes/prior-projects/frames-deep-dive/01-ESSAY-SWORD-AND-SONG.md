# The Sword and the Song

## An Essay on *Novus Ordo Seclorum*, *Opus Dei*, and the Difference Between True and False Masons

---

### I. Two Phrases, and Why They Came to Us Wearing Masks

There are two Latin phrases that have spent the last century being whispered about rather than read: *novus ordo seclorum*, stamped on the back of the American one-dollar bill beneath an unfinished pyramid and an eye, and *opus dei*, the name of a Catholic institution — and, before that, the name of something far older.

Both phrases are real. Both are documented. Both are older than the stories told about them. And both, in our time, function less as words than as loaded dice: pick them up and they come down showing whatever the thrower wants.

*Novus ordo seclorum* means, precisely and grammatically, "a new order of the ages." It is a line from Virgil's Fourth Eclogue — *magnus ab integro saeclorum nascitur ordo*, "the great cycle of the ages is born anew" — written by a Roman poet in 40 BC, in the atmosphere of the peace of Brundisium, as a song about a golden age returning. When Charles Thomson, Secretary of the Continental Congress, set it beneath the pyramid in 1782, he wrote down what he meant by it in his own hand: the words "signify the beginning of the new American Æra, which commences from that date." The eye above the pyramid alluded, in his words, "to the many signal interpositions of providence in favour of the American cause." That is the whole of the official meaning. The United States government has published this explanation for over two hundred years.

*Opus Dei* means "the work of God." In the oldest Christian usage it names the daily round of prayer — the Divine Office — and the Rule of St. Benedict, written in the sixth century, commands the monk to let nothing be preferred to it: *nihil Operi Dei praeponatur*. It is one of the most beautiful phrases in Latin Christendom, and it was already ancient when the institution took it as a name in 1928, when a Spanish priest named Josemaría Escrivá founded a movement in Madrid for the sanctification of ordinary work. That institution is real, public, canonically defined, and governed by the Vatican; Pope John Paul II made it a personal prelature in 1982, and Pope Francis restructured it in 2022, moving it under a new Vatican dicastery and directing that its prelate no longer be made a bishop. Its membership is self-reported at roughly eighty-five to ninety-six thousand people in more than ninety countries.

Two plain, documented, rather lovely things. And yet the world has not let them stay plain. There is a reason, and it is worth saying plainly: the people who have made a living from these phrases have spent two hundred and thirty years not reading them. And the reason the distortion worked so well is that the symbols involved — the eye, the pyramid, the brotherhood — belong to a tradition that has been under systematic attack for so long that its public record is largely written by its enemies.

### II. The Sword and the Song

Before the smears, consider what the thing actually is, in the oldest and best sense of it.

There is an old understanding that a true mason carries two things: in one hand, a sword — not for aggression but against evil, against the use of power to prey on the weak, against the corruption of the very craft he serves. And in the other hand, something like a song: the gladness of a man who is content to live, and unafraid to die. A man who has looked at the unfinished work, at the limits of life, at the fact that the temple is never quite finished in one man's lifetime — and has decided that this is acceptable, even beautiful, because he is building for something longer than himself.

That is the true thing. It is a discipline of character, not a costume. It is measured in the square of honesty, the level of equality, the plumb of uprightness — old tools, old words, all meaning the same simple things: don't lie, don't take more than your share, don't bow to the tyrant, and help the man who is down even when nobody is watching. A true mason is not a man who belongs to a secret; he is a man who belongs to a standard. The sword is real, but it is drawn only against evil. The song is real, and it is the reason the sword never turns into a weapon of ambition: a man who is glad to live and not afraid to die cannot be bought, cannot be frightened, and cannot be made to serve a false king.

Now consider what has been done to that image.

Beginning in the 1790s, in the panic of the French Revolution, two books — John Robison's *Proofs of a Conspiracy* and the Abbé Barruel's *Memoirs Illustrating the History of Jacobinism* — told Europe that the Masons and the Illuminati had engineered the Revolution itself. The claim was false, but it was useful, and it has never stopped being useful. In 1826, the disappearance of a man named William Morgan in upstate New York produced an American anti-Masonic political party. In 1903, the forgery known as the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion* appeared in Russia — a document so thoroughly discredited that its status as a forgery is one of the best-documented facts in conspiracy history (the London *Times* demonstrated in 1921 that it was plagiarized from a French satire; the U.S. Senate examined it in 1964; the Holocaust Memorial Museum, the ADL, and Britannica all document it) — and yet it remains in circulation, recycled decade after decade, because it is the "smoking gun" that the narrative cannot do without. In the 1920s Nesta Webster fused the Illuminati myth with anti-communism. In 1958 the John Birch Society built a mass movement on the one-world-government reading. In 1971 Gary Allen's *None Dare Call It Conspiracy* named the Council on Foreign Relations and the Trilateral Commission as the hidden "Insiders." In 1990, President George H. W. Bush — speaking about the Gulf crisis, in a speech about a post–Cold War international order — used the phrase "a new world order," and in 1991 Pat Robertson wrote a book with that title tying the whole apparatus to end-times prophecy. In 1995 the *Left Behind* novels dramatized it for tens of millions of readers. In 2017 QAnon inherited the whole structure.

Note what this pipeline does. It takes a real institution with real flaws and real virtues — a discipline of character with a long and genuinely mixed history — and replaces it with a cartoon: a shadowy elite that controls everything, hides in plain sight, and speaks in symbols. The cartoon is convenient for many people. It is convenient for demagogues, who can blame "them" for everything. It is convenient for writers, who can sell eighty million copies of a novel in which a Catholic organization is a murderous secret society with an albino monk-assassin — the *Da Vinci Code*, published 2003, which dressed its inventions in a page claiming they were all factual, and which was answered by the real Opus Dei with point-by-point corrections: no monks, no vows, no murder, no involvement in the Vatican Bank bailout. (The novel's other pillar, the Priory of Sion, was a documented hoax invented by a French confidence artist in 1956, complete with forged archives deposited in the Bibliothèque nationale.) It is even convenient for the institutions themselves, in a strange way: the real Opus Dei's prelate admitted in 2006 that Dan Brown had "turned a profit for us," and the group's website traffic soared.

And it is convenient, above all, for the false masons.

### III. True and False

Here is the distinction that the whole noise has been designed to obscure.

A false mason — a false brother, a false anything — is not a man who has merely fallen short. He is a man who has learned the language of the true thing and put it to the service of power. He wears the symbols, speaks the words, takes the oaths, and uses the network for advantage: for wealth, for influence, for the pleasure of being inside when others are outside. He does not care what a man is. He cares what a man can do for him. The craft becomes a ladder, the lodge becomes a lobby, the secret becomes a sales pitch, the brotherhood becomes a cartel. This is not unique to masonry — it is the fate of every human mechanism, from churches to corporations to governments: the true thing is always shadowed by its counterfeit, which is why the counterfeit must be named. "There are false ones who leverage it for power just like every other mechanism."

And the deepest trick of the false ones — the thing that makes them so hard to fight — is that they *want* the conspiracy theory to be true. The cartoon of the all-powerful hidden elite is their best camouflage. If the world believes that the pyramid and the eye announce a secret world government, then no one looks at the real men doing real good and real harm in ordinary rooms; everyone is staring at a myth. The myth protects the counterfeit. The true mason is embarrassed by the myth, because it slanders the craft; the false mason is delighted by it, because it is a wall of smoke he can hide behind. When the world is taught that the sword and the song are actually a dagger and a plot, the man with the real sword and the real song becomes indistinguishable from the man with neither — and the man with neither is the one who profits.

### IV. What the Record Actually Shows

Against all this noise, the documented record is remarkably quiet and clear.

On the Great Seal: the design went through four documented committees between 1776 and 1782, and the membership question is now documented man by man. Of everyone who sat on or advised the committees, only one is *definitely* a Freemason: Benjamin Franklin — and his committee's design was rejected. Two are *definitely not*: John Adams and Charles Thomson, the man who actually produced the adopted design and wrote its official explanation. For Jefferson (research since 1976 concludes he was not a Mason), Lovell, Hopkinson, Middleton, and Rutledge there is no firm evidence either way; for du Simitière, Morin Scott, Houston, Arthur Lee, Boudinot, and Barton there are no records at all — Barton being at times confused with a different William Barton who was a Mason. The Eye of Providence was a common Christian emblem of providence long before the seal — it appears throughout Renaissance and Enlightenment emblem books — and it entered standard Masonic iconography only in 1797, fifteen years *after* the seal was adopted, with the publication of Thomas Smith Webb's *Freemason's Monitor*. The standard scholarly history, Patterson and Dougall's *The Eagle and the Shield* (1978), concludes: "It seems likely that the designers of the Great Seal and the Masons took their symbols from parallel sources, and unlikely that the seal designers consciously copied Masonic symbols with the intention of incorporating Masonic symbolism into the national coat of arms." Both were reading from the same book of eighteenth-century symbols — providence, classical poetry, the Enlightenment's shared visual language. And a Masonic researcher, S. Brent Morris, writing for the Masonic Service Association, put it directly: the Great Seal "is not a Masonic emblem, nor does it contain hidden Masonic symbols" — the eye-in-pyramid is "a uniquely American, not Masonic, icon."

And the "smoking gun" of the dollar bill — the 1935 placement of the seal's reverse on the currency — was a documented Treasury design decision: Roosevelt approved and personally edited the design, initialing the model "FDR" on June 26, 1935, and Secretary of Agriculture Henry Wallace later recalled having drawn his attention to it. No document connects any of this to any occult body, and the design itself predates the dollar bill by a century and a half. Honesty, however, requires naming the one real Masonic thread in this story: both of the men who put the seal on the dollar bill — Franklin D. Roosevelt and Henry A. Wallace — were themselves Freemasons. That is a true kernel, and the myth rides on exactly such kernels. But it cuts the other way too: a Masonic presence at the 1935 adoption does not make the 1782 design a Masonic emblem — the designers were documented non-Masons, and the design's meaning was fixed by its own documents a century and a half earlier. The layer-cake is the point: real masons handled the symbol's journey onto the currency; the false ones built the myth around it. The distinction between true and false is not about which side of the symbol's history a man stood on — it is about what he did with it.

None of this settles the deeper question, and it should not pretend to. The honest position is more interesting than either the myth or the debunk: the founders and the Masons shared a symbolic culture, and whether any Masonic influence touched Thomson's final wording is simply not answered by the accessible record. The answer, if it exists, lives in the authoritative sources — the full scholarly history, the lodge-side research archives, the papers of the men themselves — not in the speculative literature that has recycled the same claims since 1797. That is the difference between research and noise: research goes to the record; noise goes to the recycling bin.

On Opus Dei: the same pattern, one layer thinner. The "Catholic Mafia" label was in print by 1976, before the novel that made it famous. The institution itself is a public, canonically ordinary (if unusual) structure: a personal prelature of the Catholic Church, lay-led, founded to sanctify ordinary work, with members who are ordinary people in ordinary professions, and with documented internal controversies like every large human institution — including ex-member memoirs and recent investigative journalism, and including the Church's own 2022 restructuring of its governance. The wild version — a murderous secret order pulling Vatican strings — is contradicted by the record at every point. What the record does show, honestly, is that the name is ambiguous by nature: *opus Dei* names both the Church's highest act of worship and a real institution. The ambiguity is what makes the counterfeit possible.

### V. Why the Skew Persists

The documents I drew this from are themselves a study in why the noise wins.

First, the false thing explains everything with one cause. A single hidden elite accounts for the French Revolution, the Federal Reserve, the United Nations, wars, and papal politics. As Richard Hofstadter observed in 1964, the paranoid style's enemy is "a free, active, demonic agent" — and such an agent absorbs disconfirmation: denial becomes proof of concealment. The narrative cannot be falsified because falsification is itself read as evidence.

Second, it is self-referential. Each generation's texts cite the previous generation's — Robison to Webster to Allen to Robertson to Epperson to the internet — so the corpus looks like a documented tradition even though it is a chain of citations with no anchor except a forged document. The *Protocols* are the anchor, and their documented forgery is itself reinterpreted as part of the plot.

Third, it rides real facts. The Illuminati existed. The seal really does carry an eye, a pyramid, and Latin. Bush really said "new world order." Opus Dei really is secretive by reputation. The Priory of Sion documents really sit in the Bibliothèque nationale. Every true kernel gives the surrounding falsehood an aura of documentation.

Fourth, it has institutional carriers with economic and identity incentives: prophecy publishing, the John Birch tradition, militia movements, the internet's attention economy, and — above all — fiction. Eighty million copies of a novel is a more effective propaganda machine than any tract ever written.

And fifth — the point that matters most here — it satisfies a demand for moral clarity. The world is messy: banks are opaque, churches are imperfect, governments lie, and institutions of every kind contain both true men and false ones. The conspiracy frame converts that mess into a legible morality play with identifiable villains and, in the evangelical version, a guaranteed ending. The frame's audience is not consuming evidence; it is consuming narrative satisfaction. And the false masons — the power-leveragers of every stripe — are happy to be cast as the villains, because the casting makes them look like the masters.

### VI. The Song Survives the Smoke

So what is a reasonable man to do with these two phrases?

Read them. That is the whole of it. Read Virgil: *magnus ab integro saeclorum nascitur ordo* — a song, written in the year 40 BC, about a world beginning again, and taken up in 1782 by a man who believed his young nation was that new beginning. Read the Rule of St. Benedict: *nihil Operi Dei praeponatur* — let nothing be preferred to the work of God; an instruction to sixth-century monks about prayer, and the origin of a name that a twentieth-century priest chose because he believed the work was God's, not his. The meanings are plain, documented, and human. They do not require an eye to decode; they require only the willingness to read what the men who wrote them said they meant.

And when the whisperers come — and they will always come, because the noise is profitable — the answer is the same answer the true mason has always had: *show the record*. Not the recycling. The record. The signatures, the letters, the official explanations, the full scholarly histories, the archives. The sword against evil is drawn precisely there: against the lie, the smear, the manufactured mystery — the false ones who would have you believe that a song is a plot and a standard is a conspiracy.

The true thing survives the smoke because it was never made of smoke. It is made of character, which is invisible to the myth-makers; of work, which is unglamorous; of the square, the level, the plumb — old tools for old virtues, unimpressive to everyone except the man who has tried to live by them. A man who is glad to live and not afraid to die does not need the world to fear him. He needs only to be able to look at his own work in the evening and find it true.

The temple is unfinished. It always has been. The eye above the pyramid looks on; the song is sung in the meantime; the sword stays ready against the false — and the building goes on, one true stone at a time, under a motto that means what it has always meant: a new order of the ages, begun in 1776, still being built, and never owned by the myth.
