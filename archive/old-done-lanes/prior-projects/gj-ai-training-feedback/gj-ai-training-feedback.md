# Feedback on the "Responsible AI Use" Training

*Prepared for the AI Council lead · 12 August 2026*

The core of the course is right, and two parts are genuinely strong: the rule that approval status — not accessibility, not blocking, not a personal device — decides what may be used, and the "if you would not post it publicly, don't enter it into a non-approved tool" test. Keep those. The problem is that the course doesn't do what it teaches. It was produced with AI tools, never says so, and shows no sign of the human review it demands of employees — and that is the first thing employees will check, because it is exactly the transparency the course asks of them. It is also already behind its moment: it dates generative AI to "the 2000 and 20s" when the verifiable anchor is November 2022 (ChatGPT, one million users in five days); it never mentions labeling AI-generated content — now required by law in the EU and California for those in scope, and increasingly required by client contracts; it frames AI as something you type into while agents now act on employees' behalf across the enterprise; and it has no version or refresh cadence, so it will keep drifting from the tools and rules it governs. Fix the anchors, add the disclosure rule, extend the accountability message to agents, put the course on a review cycle — and start with the one change that matters most: acknowledge in the course that the course itself was made with AI and reviewed by people. That one sentence would make the training the best example of its own rules.
